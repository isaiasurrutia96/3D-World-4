# LetsGo Planning Handoff

This is a planning handoff for Codex, so that Codex can write the LetsGo master plan later. It is **not** the master plan, and it does not choose a build order: the owner is still adding ideas and asked to hold off on the plan (Q15). It was written by the "LetsGo Plan" Claude chat on 24 November 2026 from the repo at commit `5689bb2` (origin/main), the report folder `docs/plan/` on branch `claude/nice-cannon-50ao74` (not on main), and the owner's own words in the Claude chats. Every claim has a source. Where approval or facts could not be confirmed, the item says "unclear" and why.

## How to use this

1. Section A is the owner's vision and feel goals. The labels Q1 to Q17 are the owner's statements in the LetsGo Plan chat, quoted in A4.
2. Section B is the decision ledger. An approved item changes only after asking the owner ([HOW-TO-WORK-WITH-ME.md](HOW-TO-WORK-WITH-ME.md) section D2, lines 77-82).
3. The [plan/](plan/README.md) files are Claude's analysis, not owner decisions, unless they quote the owner. They exist only on branch `claude/nice-cannon-50ao74`, so those links work only there until it is merged. "plan/NN" means `plan/NN-*.md` (for example, plan/05 is `plan/05-MINIATURE-ISLAND-RESEARCH.md`). "An" means `plan/appendix/An-*.md`: A1 diagnostic findings, A3 going-private research, A4 movement plan check, A5 miniature-island research.
4. Line numbers are for main at `5689bb2`. On branch `claude/nice-cannon-50ao74`, [HOW-TO-WORK-WITH-ME.md](HOW-TO-WORK-WITH-ME.md) has 7 extra lines from section B on and 11 extra lines from section D on (so B 43-48 becomes 50-55, D2 77-82 becomes 88-93, and G 97-106 becomes 108-117), and [IDEAS-INDEX.md](IDEAS-INDEX.md) has 2 extra lines after line 15 (line 52 becomes 54). ## **A. The owner's vision**

### A1. What LetsGo is

1. LetsGo is a 3D island game built around one athletic, chunky, ragdoll-style mannequin. Players explore one connected world of themed islands on foot, by kart, by skateboard and by swimming. They race karts in three classes (S Kart, also called GoKart; GT; F1), play about 20 small sports and arcade minigames, win coins, prizes and gear, and return to a home island they can build on ([LETSGO-MASTER-PLAN-NOTES.md](LETSGO-MASTER-PLAN-NOTES.md) 7; 15 feature cards in [FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md) and 20 game cards in [MINIGAMES.md](MINIGAMES.md), approved 19 Nov).
2. All of this is approved design on paper, and none of it is playable yet. The avatar, kart and island have never been in one world, the avatar cannot walk, and there is no race with a finish line ([plan/01](plan/01-DIAGNOSTIC-REPORT.md) 19-22). Online play comes only after a solid single-player game ([FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md) 345-346). Selling comes later by wrapping the web game for Steam and the phone stores, then consoles; it must run on a phone by link and on the owner's 2015/2016 MacBook ([NEXT.md](NEXT.md) 46-60).
3. On 23-24 Nov the owner changed how the game will be built. A miniature comes first: one island big enough for at least two tracks per class (two each, kept simple), a small skate park and a practice field with a tiny station for every minigame (Q4). The code restarts cleanly, but not from zero, and every idea is kept (Q7). The big world follows once the miniature works. These decisions are recorded only in [plan/06](plan/06-DECISIONS-AND-OPEN-QUESTIONS.md) 8-17.

### A2. How it should feel

1. Feel matters most, and two pillars must survive the restart: "the movement of the ragdoll and how the fundamental movements are going to be able to adapt to every other movement that's needed, as well as the goals I have for the driving feeling too" (Q6).
2. The avatar is a rigid, jointed mannequin with visible ball joints, chunky clay, heavy strong arms and light feet, with no sharp edges. The owner chose rigid over a skinned mesh so throws and shots would "look so, like, different than what any other game shows" (Official, 20 Nov 19:49-19:53 UTC; [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md) 19, 63).
3. He moves like "an athletic person fully in tune with their body; every movement purposeful" ([../motion-core/PLAN.md](../motion-core/PLAN.md) 15): deliberate feet, real weight shift, no shuffle or sliding ([AVATAR-DIRECTION.md](AVATAR-DIRECTION.md) 3-10). Movement grows from a foundation (Q8): "imagine this avatar had the natural bodily intelligence of any sport, and combine that into one person". The older reference is "Super Mario 64 as if it were made today" ([NEXT.md](NEXT.md) 5), and running may look "kind of like Super Mario" (line 424; approved 20 Nov as decision 43b, but whether it survived the 20 Nov reset is unclear, see B8.7). The owner judges by eye: he called the 20 Nov walk "absolutely terrible" and asked to "erase and forget everything" (lines 448, 484-485).
4. Sports should feel real: no aiming guide lines, and controls chosen after the owner tests, "maybe not the traditional way" ([MINIGAMES.md](MINIGAMES.md) 25-30). He wants no two throws to land the same and none of the usual power windup or aim guide: "I kind of don't want none of that" (Official, 23 Nov 06:26 UTC).
5. Driving keeps the quick, approachable steering of the old LetsGo race ([KART-DESIGN.md](KART-DESIGN.md) 5). In the 23 Nov wording, S Kart is the most arcade, GT sits between, and F1 is fastest with a smooth, grippy arc ([LETSGO-MASTER-PLAN-NOTES.md](LETSGO-MASTER-PLAN-NOTES.md) 45-47); the last owner-approved wording is the 21 Nov one in [KARTS.md](KARTS.md) 16-20 (see B5.3). His hands-on feedback leans arcade: turns felt too realistic, so grip was doubled, and he enjoyed the 180 mph boost more ([../racing-core/PLAN.md](../racing-core/PLAN.md) 187, 386-393).
6. The world is one connected place where nearby islands load as he gets close ([FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md) 119-126), and the watch is a shortcut with "no menus or loading screens" (line 22). Skyline Crossing's stacked, colorful city is the leading visual reference, a block behaves the same in every game ("grass is grass"), and the banked oval is a favorite ([LETSGO-MASTER-PLAN-NOTES.md](LETSGO-MASTER-PLAN-NOTES.md) 14-27; [WORLD-RULES.md](WORLD-RULES.md) 15-17; [NEXT.md](NEXT.md) 30-33).

### A3. What the owner cares about most

1. A working game first, business and legal later, and nobody stealing the idea (Q2).
2. An orderly plan like a skyscraper's (Q1), tricks that make it easy for one person (Q3), and no wasted usage ([../CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) section B item 3).
3. Very solid, tested code ([HOW-TO-WORK-WITH-ME.md](HOW-TO-WORK-WITH-ME.md) section G, lines 97-106). For the avatar, pictures come first, then "once i am 100% satisfied you will then rebuild that exact avatar in solid code" ([HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md) 94).
4. The number-one rule: "Before you answer, recommend, or build anything, research the best ways to do it" ([HOW-TO-WORK-WITH-ME.md](HOW-TO-WORK-WITH-ME.md) section A, lines 21-26), plus a double-check of every step (Q5, Q9).
5. Every idea recorded with its status ([IDEAS-INDEX.md](IDEAS-INDEX.md) 3-4).
6. Flexibility without surprises: nothing is permanent and he wants to be challenged, but approved parts never change without asking, nothing starts before "Go", and honesty is the only fixed commitment ([HOW-TO-WORK-WITH-ME.md](HOW-TO-WORK-WITH-ME.md) sections B, D2, E and F, lines 43-48, 77-95).
7. Fast phone testing by link, with no laptop (Q12), and no long waits: "I don't want to wait 10 minutes after you finish your response or you give me the link" (Official, 23 Nov 06:50 UTC).
8. Plain English, teaching as you go, short "audio style" replies while driving (Q11). He does not use Blender: "I just have the ideas ... you're gonna have to ... guide me and pick the best recommendation for also my situation" (Kart, 22 Nov 05:58 UTC).

## **D. What depends on what**

Facts only; no build order.

### D1. Dependencies

1. The movement rig depends on a final body: motion-core fits the old 1.8945 m avatar, and the spine or hips choice changes the piece count, including the 17-piece physics body ([../motion-core/skeleton/bones.js](../motion-core/skeleton/bones.js) 70; [research/inventing-movement/REPORT.md](research/inventing-movement/REPORT.md) 127).
2. Human joint ranges depend on the owner allowing shape changes. Today the waist cannot lean, the chest bends 15° and the collarbone 4° ([plan/04](plan/04-MOVEMENT-PLAN.md) 28-29).
3. Walking depends on the speed decision; it has been built and removed five to seven times ([plan/01](plan/01-DIAGNOSTIC-REPORT.md) 64). Walking the island also needs height, gravity, jump and world collision, because the controller has no y axis ([../motion-core/controller.js](../motion-core/controller.js) 1, 18).
4. The cockpit fit depends on the 1,930 mm base and stock geometry; it used a 1,894 mm pose ([KART-DESIGN.md](KART-DESIGN.md) 48, 56). Getting in and steering need agreed seat, wheel and pedal data ([../racing-core/PLAN.md](../racing-core/PLAN.md) 85).
5. Class handling depends on the simulated wheelbase. GT3 V3 and the F1 presets were tuned on 2.10 m and 3.04 m, not the stock 1.98 m and 2.65 m ([HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md) 150-151; [../racing-core/kart/prototypes.ts](../racing-core/kart/prototypes.ts) 37-53).
6. Island tracks depend on a loader beyond the four fixed tracks, an authoring validator, grass runoff, the owner's road-width and second-track answers, and Codex recording the authorization (racing decision 7, [../racing-core/PLAN.md](../racing-core/PLAN.md) 38; A5 385). Road width alone moves the island from about 390 x 300 m to 480 x 375 m ([plan/05](plan/05-MINIATURE-ISLAND-RESEARCH.md) 15-22).
7. Ground over about 220 m depends on new storage. VoxelWorld's one Map holds 16,777,216 cells, one layer of a 220 m square; the island needs about 49.9 million per layer, an 86 m layer used 122 MB, and keys wrap past ±440 m on z ([../src/world/collision.ts](../src/world/collision.ts) 17-35; A5 1779-1784).
8. Ramps need wedge and smooth collision; every block is a full cube today ([../src/world/world.ts](../src/world/world.ts) 75-80).
9. Stations depend on a ball system, a challenge runner, aim input, the owner's ball numbers and later throw and kick moves. The research ball kernel had false baskets and wall leaks (A5 740-801).
10. Pins, ragdoll and hits depend on the physics choice (B9.3), a decision on adding a physics library (B9.4), an iPhone test and balance. Rapier is proposed only when the first station that needs tumbling objects, bowling pins, is built ([plan/05](plan/05-MINIATURE-ISLAND-RESEARCH.md) 45-49); the hit system needs balance first ([FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md) 315-336); a Rapier-versus-Jolt phone test is proposed ([research/inventing-movement/REPORT.md](research/inventing-movement/REPORT.md) 147-153).
11. Any class race depends on per-kart geometry in multi-kart contacts ([../racing-core/contacts/karts.ts](../racing-core/contacts/karts.ts) 41-46). A first race needs sections 4-5 and a boost limit ([../racing-core/PLAN.md](../racing-core/PLAN.md) 79-80; [../racing-core/kart/index.ts](../racing-core/kart/index.ts) 173-177). Ghost saves need a new module: the old one allows 8 million characters, above a browser origin's roughly 5.24 million, shared with the owner's other github.io sites (A5 1928-1930).
12. Karts hitting block structures depends on Claude's pending adapter ([../racing-core/kart/README.md](../racing-core/kart/README.md) 48). One world page depends on the rule 16 decision ([../CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) 76-79) and racing section 8 ([../racing-core/PLAN.md](../racing-core/PLAN.md) 83).
