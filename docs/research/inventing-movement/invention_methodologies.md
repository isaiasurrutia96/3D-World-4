# Proven Methodologies for Inventing a New System When No Existing Solution Is Available

Scope note for the report writer: these notes cover general engineering, design and scientific invention methods, not game-specific ones. Evidence strength is tagged as **[Measured]** (controlled experiment, RCT or meta-analysis), **[Measured, small/student]** (controlled but small or student samples), **[Case history]** (documented historical or organisational case), or **[Anecdotal]** (workshop folklore, interviews, practitioner claims). Full-text fetches of several primary papers failed in this session because these hosts were blocked by the network sandbox: hci.stanford.edu, invention.psychology.msstate.edu, sloanreview.mit.edu, www.lrdc.pitt.edu, www.gwern.net. Findings below therefore come from abstracts, publisher pages, and secondary summaries surfaced by search; exact effect sizes were usually not verifiable.

## 1. What are the established methods for inventing a novel system, and how is each applied step by step?

### Takeaway
There are about ten well-documented approaches. They split into **framing methods** (first principles, TRIZ contradictions, morphological analysis), **idea-source methods** (analogy and biomimicry), **process frameworks** (design thinking or the Double Diamond, set-based concurrent engineering, research through design), and **learning loops** (the scientific hypothesis-experiment loop, build-measure-learn, the Wright brothers' isolate-and-measure approach, Schön's reflection-in-action). The methods with the strongest documented invention record (the Wrights, Toyota, Edison) all broke the problem into functions and tested each function with a cheap, purpose-built rig before committing to a whole design.

### Cited Findings

#### 1a. First-principles reasoning (decompose to fundamentals, rebuild)
- Definition: Aristotle described a first principle as the "first basis from which a thing is known". The modern engineering heuristic (popularised by Elon Musk) borrows this label loosely — [CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2019/10/12/elon-musk-borrowed-problem-solving-skill-aristotle/).
- Canonical example **[Anecdotal]**: in a 2012 interview Musk argued that instead of accepting the industry figure of about $600 per kWh for battery packs, one should price the raw constituent materials, which came to roughly $80 per kWh. Secondary sources say Gigafactory costs later fell to about $200 per kWh. These figures come from popular press, not audited data — [Quartz](https://qz.com/972118/simple-math-is-why-elon-musks-companies-keep-doing-what-others-dont-even-consider-possible/); [CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2019/10/12/elon-musk-borrowed-problem-solving-skill-aristotle/).
- Steps as practised (from the same sources): (1) state the assumption or the accepted solution; (2) break it into its physical or logical constituents; (3) find the hard limits for each (cost, physics); (4) rebuild a solution from those constituents, ignoring the conventional form — [Quartz](https://qz.com/972118/simple-math-is-why-elon-musks-companies-keep-doing-what-others-dont-even-consider-possible/).
- Bradshaw's analysis of invention notes that in the Wright brothers' case (and in his rocket comparison), "search was avoided ... through the use of theory": reasoning from aerodynamic fundamentals cut down the number of designs that had to be tried — [Bradshaw, ALT 2005 lecture abstract](https://www-alg.ist.hokudai.ac.jp/~thomas/ALT05/gary.html).
- Evidence quality: the search found no peer-reviewed controlled study of "first-principles thinking" as a named invention method; the support is anecdotal and historical — [search summary of CleanTechnica / Quartz coverage](https://cleantechnica.com/2019/10/12/elon-musk-borrowed-problem-solving-skill-aristotle/).

#### 1b. Analogical transfer and biomimicry (borrow solutions from other fields or nature)
- Two documented entry points in biologically inspired design: **problem-driven** (start from the design problem and search biology for analogues) and **solution-driven** (start from a biological phenomenon and look for an application). Helms, Vattam and Goel (2009, *Design Studies*) observed both in a biologically inspired design course, along with "regular patterns of practice" and errors that recur — [Georgia Tech DILab](https://dilab.gatech.edu/publications/biologically-inspired-design-process-and-products); [AI EDAM protocol study citing it](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ai-edam/article/design-problem-solving-with-biological-analogies-a-verbal-protocol-study/F69BBEE77460DA27C40972AD03C10C21).
- Recurring errors catalogued from that work: vaguely defined problems, poor problem-solution pairing, solution fixation (general to all conceptual design), and, specific to biomimicry, using "off-the-shelf" biological solutions, misapplied analogy, and improper analogical transfer — [AI EDAM, citing Helms et al.](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ai-edam/article/design-problem-solving-with-biological-analogies-a-verbal-protocol-study/F69BBEE77460DA27C40972AD03C10C21).
- Case history **[Case history]**: the Shinkansen 500-series nose. Trains leaving tunnels produced a loud pressure "boom". Engineer Eiji Nakatsu, a birdwatcher, related the sudden change of air resistance entering a tunnel to a kingfisher diving into water with little splash, and the team tested nose shapes in a scale-model tunnel. The 500 series entered service on 22 March 1997. Trellis reports a 30% drop in air resistance and a measured 13% reduction in power versus the 300 series; popular sources cite "10% faster, 15% less electricity" (figures conflict) — [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/how-one-engineers-birdwatching-made-japans-bullet-train-better); [Lafayette biomimicry page](https://sites.lafayette.edu/biomimicry/?p=94).
- Note on that case: the analogy supplied a candidate shape, but the decision was made by systematic scale-model testing — [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/how-one-engineers-birdwatching-made-japans-bullet-train-better).
- Steps as practised (synthesised from the above): define the function needed in neutral terms (e.g., "enter a denser medium without a pressure spike"); search a source domain for systems that perform that function; map the underlying mechanism (not the surface form); transfer and adapt; test — [AI EDAM](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ai-edam/article/design-problem-solving-with-biological-analogies-a-verbal-protocol-study/F69BBEE77460DA27C40972AD03C10C21); [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/how-one-engineers-birdwatching-made-japans-bullet-train-better).

#### 1c. TRIZ (Altshuller's theory of inventive problem solving)
- Origin: developed by Genrich Altshuller and associates from 1946 by analysing patterns of invention in the global patent literature. He found that problems that could be modelled as the same kind of engineering contradiction tended to share conceptually similar solutions, which were distilled into **40 inventive principles** — [WIPO TISC TRIZ toolkit](https://www.wipo.int/export/sites/www/tisc/en/docs/tisc-toolkit-triz-description.pdf); [Wikipedia: 40 principles of invention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40_principles_of_invention).
- The contradiction matrix has **39 generic engineering parameters** on both axes. Steps: (1) state the problem as a contradiction ("improving parameter X worsens parameter Y"); (2) translate X and Y into the nearest of the 39 generic parameters; (3) read the matrix cell, which lists the principles that most often resolved that contradiction in patents; (4) interpret each suggested principle for the specific case. The matrix is not symmetric, and empty cells mean no principle is statistically favoured — [MATRIZ wiki: contradiction matrix](https://wiki.matriz.org/docs/triz/problem-solving-tools-5890/contradictions/engineering-contradiction-5995/contradiction-matrix-6026/).
- One secondary source claims Altshuller found only about 1,250 typical system conflicts; that figure appeared in a single source and is unconfirmed — [Wikipedia: 40 principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40_principles_of_invention) (via search summary).
- Practitioner reality: the Inventive Principles are the most popular TRIZ technique; practitioners report difficulties ranging from the method's complexity to organisational and cultural barriers, and there are no clear rules for choosing which TRIZ tool to use — [Ilevbare, Probert & Phaal 2013, *Technovation* 33(2-3):30-37](https://typeset.io/papers/a-review-of-triz-and-its-benefits-and-challenges-in-practice-24hqatxxin).

#### 1d. Design thinking and the Double Diamond (IDEO, Stanford d.school, UK Design Council)
- The Double Diamond was popularised by the UK Design Council in 2005 (one Council page says 2004), adapted from Béla Banathy's 1996 divergence-convergence model. Phases: **Discover** (understand the problem rather than assume it, spend time with affected people), **Define** (reframe the challenge from those insights), **Develop** (generate many different answers, seek inspiration elsewhere, co-design), **Deliver** (test solutions at small scale, reject those that fail, improve those that work). The Council stresses it is not linear. It was refreshed in 2019 as the "Framework for Innovation" — [Design Council: Framework for Innovation](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/framework-for-innovation); [Wikipedia: Double Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Diamond_(design_process_model)).
- Liedtka's explanation of why it works: people's intrinsic biases and habits inhibit imagination, and design-thinking practices mitigate "the human biases that thwart innovation" — [IDEO summary of Liedtka 2018](https://designthinking.ideo.com/resources/why-design-thinking-works); [O'Reilly / HBR chapter](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/hbrs-10-must/9781633698819/ch02.html).

#### 1e. The scientific method / hypothesis-experiment loop
- In the Camuffo et al. RCT (below), treatment founders were trained to treat their business idea as a theory, derive hypotheses, and test them, while controls relied on intuition; the approach is argued to reduce false positives and false negatives in decisions — [Management Science supplement, Camuffo et al. 2020](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3249); [Bocconi news](https://www.unibocconi.it/en/news/entrepreneurs-who-think-scientists-get-better-results).
- Invention example **[Case history]**: the Wrights' 1900-1901 gliders produced only about one-third of the lift predicted by Otto Lilienthal's tables. They first ran a crude **bicycle-wheel test rig** comparing their model against the prediction, which confirmed the discrepancy, then built a wind tunnel (tests September to December 1901) — [NASA Glenn: 1901 results](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/K-12/airplane/wrights/results.html); [NASA Glenn: 1901 bicycle tests](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/1901-bicycle-tests).

#### 1f. Research through design (RtD) and Schön's reflection-in-action
- Zimmerman, Forlizzi and Evenson (CHI 2007, pp. 493-502) propose a model where designers make artifacts that integrate existing knowledge "in an attempt to make the right thing"; the artifact itself is the research contribution. They provide four lenses for evaluating such contributions, building on Frayling's (1993) "research through design" — [Zimmerman et al. 2007 PDF](https://courses.ischool.berkeley.edu/i262/s13/readings_pdf/Zimmerman_Research_Through_Design_as_a_Method_for_IaD_in_HCI_0.pdf); [bibliographic record](https://bibtex.github.io/CHI-2007-ZimmermanFE.html).
- A later paper by Forlizzi, Koskinen, Hekkert and Zimmerman, citing the 2007 paper, says constructive design research (RtD) has become "an accepted and popular mode of scholarly inquiry" in design research and HCI (quote seen only in a search summary; the later paper's own URL was not retrieved) — [UCSB copy of an RtD reading](https://creativetech.mat.ucsb.edu/readings/Research_through_Design.pdf).
- Schön (1983 *The Reflective Practitioner*; 1992 "Designing as reflective conversation with the materials of a design situation"): designing is a conversation in which each move gets "backtalk" from the medium; surprises trigger **reflection-in-action** (adjusting in the moment), distinct from reflection-on-action afterwards. Schön: "it is rare that the designer has the design all in her head in advance, and then merely translates it" — [Stanford "Bringing Design to Software" interview with Schön](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/bds/9-schon.html); [Schön 1992 PDF](https://www.uio.no/studier/emner/matnat/ifi/IN1060/v25/pensum/schon_design-conversation.pdf).
- The distinction between reflection-in-action and reflection on reflection-in-action remains unclear to many readers — [DRS 2022 paper](https://dl.designresearchsociety.org/drs-conference-papers/drs2022/researchpapers/89).

#### 1g. Morphological analysis (Zwicky box)
- Developed by Fritz Zwicky (Caltech astrophysicist), applied by him to astronomy and jet/rocket propulsion, for "multi-dimensional, non-quantified complex problems" where causal modelling and simulation do not work well — [Wikipedia: Morphological analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphological_analysis_(problem-solving)).
- Tom Ritchey (Swedish Defence Research Agency) extended it as General Morphological Analysis (GMA) with computer support: a method for "structuring and investigating the total set of relationships" in a problem complex — [Ritchey 2006, JORS, via Academia](https://www.academia.edu/715679/Problem_structuring_using_computer_aided_morphological_analysis); [Ritchey GMA paper (archive.org)](https://ia600207.us.archive.org/27/items/ritchey_swemorph_Gma/gma.pdf).
- Steps: (1) identify the independent dimensions (parameters) of the problem; (2) list the possible values of each; (3) lay them out as a morphological chart ("Zwicky box"), the "morphospace"; (4) through "existential combinatorics" synthesise a solution space by combining one value per dimension and screening out inconsistent combinations — [Technological Forecasting & Social Change 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/tefoso/v126y2018icp81-91.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphological_analysis_(problem-solving)).

#### 1h. Set-based concurrent engineering (Toyota)
- Sobek, Ward and Liker (1999, *Sloan Management Review*), from case study of Toyota's product development: start with a wide range of candidate solutions and narrow them gradually; the first principle is to **map the design space** — [Sloan Management Review](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/toyotas-principles-of-setbased-concurrent-engineering); [Toche et al. 2020 review](https://publications.polymtl.ca/9280/1/2020_Toche_Set-based_design_review_new_directions.pdf).
- Mechanism: each specialist group brings a **set** of feasible options and the groups overlap their sets to find where all are feasible, instead of repeatedly criticising and changing one point design (Liker et al. 1996) — [Toche et al. 2020 review](https://publications.polymtl.ca/9280/1/2020_Toche_Set-based_design_review_new_directions.pdf).
- Precursor: Ward, Liker, Cristiano and Sobek (1995) "The Second Toyota Paradox: How Delaying Decisions Can Make Better Cars Faster" — [copy in the USMC University John Boyd collection](https://www.usmcu.edu/Portals/218/HD/Digitized%20Collections/COLL_2062%20John%20Boyd/COLL%202062_John%20Boyd_Box%2011/110305-The%20Second%20Toyota%20Paradox%20How%20Delaying%20Decisions%20Can%20Make%20Better%20Cars%20Faster%20Sloan%20Management%20Review%20Spring%201995%20with%20attached%20Note.PDF).
- The principles have stayed largely the same in later set-based design work (Ghosh & Seering 2014) — [Toche et al. 2020](https://publications.polymtl.ca/9280/1/2020_Toche_Set-based_design_review_new_directions.pdf).

#### 1i. The Wright brothers' approach (identify the critical subproblem, build a rig for each function, measure)
- They concluded early (wing-warping kite, Dayton, 1899) that "the final problem to be solved was control" — i.e., they picked **control** as the critical unsolved subproblem while others focused on power or inherent stability — [NPS: Road to First Flight](https://www.nps.gov/articles/roadtofirstflight.htm).
- Sequence **[Case history]**: 1899 kite (tests wing-warping alone) → 1900 glider (165 sq ft, forward elevator) → 1901 glider (lift shortfall, "peculiar feeling of instability" in turns) → bicycle-wheel rig → wind tunnel with home-built lift and drag balances, 100-200 models tested (Sept-Dec 1901) → 1902 glider (long thin wings, rudder coupled to wing-warping after sideslip problems; 300+ flights; first aircraft with active control on all three axes) → 1903 powered flight — [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/roadtofirstflight.htm); [Centennial of Flight, 1901 wind tunnel](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080917114018/http://centennialofflight.gov/essay/Wright_Bros/1901/WR3.htm); [NASA Glenn: unpowered aircraft](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/unpowered-aircraft); [wright-brothers.org](https://wright-brothers.org/History_Wing/Wright_Story/Inventing_the_Airplane/Not_Within_A_Thousand_Years/Not_Within_A_Thousand_Years.htm).
- Bradshaw's cognitive analysis: invention can be modelled as heuristic search, and airworthy designs are rare in the design space. Competitors used a **design-and-test** strategy (build a whole craft, try it), but field tests of a whole craft return only **global performance** that does not tell the inventor which feature to change — [Bradshaw, "Invention and Artificial Intelligence" (Springer)](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/11563983_1.pdf); [Bradshaw, "The Design and Test Strategy of Invention" (MSU)](https://invention.psychology.msstate.edu/Tale_of_Airplane/Design.html).
- A Cognitive Science Society paper on the same topic argues that searching in **function space** (lift, control, propulsion separately) is far more efficient than searching in design space — [Cognitive Science Society article](https://journalpub.escholarship.org/cognitivesciencesociety/article/32905/galley/23965/download).
- Data conflict on why Lilienthal's tables misled them: Centennial of Flight says the tables were largely correct and the error was Smeaton's coefficient; NASA says the data were accurate for Lilienthal's own configuration but could not be applied to a different wing, and the Wrights had not corrected for aspect ratio — [Centennial of Flight](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080917114018/http://centennialofflight.gov/essay/Wright_Bros/1901/WR3.htm); [NASA Glenn](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/K-12/airplane/wrights/results.html).

#### 1j. Build-measure-learn / Lean Startup, and Edison's organised experimentation
- The strongest evidence for the hypothesis-driven, build-to-learn approach in new ventures is the Camuffo et al. RCT series (see section 2) — [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3249); [CEPR DP15972](https://cepr.org/publications/dp15972).
- Edison **[Case history]**: Menlo Park was turned into what the Edison Papers editors call the first true research and development facility, with up to about sixty experimenters, machinists and others. Historian Paul Israel argues Edison was one of the first to understand that "the invention process could be organized" — [Edison Papers, Rutgers](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biographical-essays/lighting/from-research-to-development); [MIT Technology Review 1997](https://www.technologyreview.com/1997/02/01/237382/unlocking-the-legacies-of-the-edison-archives/).
- Filament search: experiments narrowed to grasses and canes (hemp, palmetto, bamboo) chosen for long uniform fibres — a systematic screen across a defined material set rather than random trial — [NPS Edison handbook](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/hh/edis/edisc2.htm); [Edison Papers](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biographical-essays/lighting/from-research-to-development).
- Development continued straight into production: by summer 1880 staff were setting up a Menlo Park lamp factory for 1,200 lamps per day — [Edison Papers](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biographical-essays/lighting/from-research-to-development).
- Caveat: some historians say Menlo Park was not a technical laboratory of the later corporate type, and as of 1997 only about a third of Edison's notebooks had been catalogued, so the popular "pure trial and error" image is not well grounded — [NPS handbook](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/hh/edis/edisc2.htm); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/1997/02/01/237382/unlocking-the-legacies-of-the-edison-archives/).

### Inferences
- The methods sort into roles rather than competitors: TRIZ, first principles and morphological analysis **frame and enumerate** the problem; analogy and biomimicry **supply candidate mechanisms**; design thinking, set-based engineering and RtD **organise the process**; the scientific loop, the Wright approach and build-measure-learn **decide what survives**.
- The Wrights' method is the closest historical match to inventing a character movement system: a dynamic, actively controlled, human-in-the-loop system where "control" (responsiveness and balance under player input) is the critical subproblem, just as it was for flight. Their sequence of single-function rigs (kite for warping, bicycle rig and tunnel for lift, glider for coupled control) maps to building isolated test harnesses for each movement function (e.g., foot placement, balance recovery, turning, input-to-motion mapping) before a full controller.
- Bradshaw's point that whole-system tests return only global metrics transfers directly: a full playtest says "it feels floaty" but not which parameter causes it; per-function rigs with numeric readouts are what make diagnosis possible.
- Morphological analysis fits movement design naturally: dimensions such as "how the body is driven" (kinematic, physics-based, hybrid), "balance strategy", "foot contact model", and "input mapping" can be laid out as a Zwicky box, then screened for incompatible combinations.
- TRIZ contradictions transfer as a framing device: "improving responsiveness worsens physical plausibility" or "improving stability worsens agility" are textbook technical contradictions; the TRIZ principles (segmentation, prior action, dynamics, feedback, etc.) can be used as prompts even without the formal matrix.

### Gaps
- The full list and exact wording of Sobek, Ward and Liker's three principles and sub-principles could not be read (sloanreview.mit.edu blocked). From secondary sources only "map the design space" and the set-intersection mechanism were confirmed.
- Zimmerman et al.'s four evaluation lenses were not named in the retrieved text.
- No primary text for Eric Ries's build-measure-learn loop (*The Lean Startup*, 2011) or Tim Brown/IDEO's process description was retrieved; descriptions of those loops here rely on the Design Council and Camuffo sources.
- Bradshaw's book chapter "The airplane and the logic of invention" itself was not retrieved; his argument is reconstructed from his related papers and lecture abstract.
- No peer-reviewed source was found that tests first-principles reasoning as a distinct invention method.

## 2. What empirical evidence or documented case histories show each method works?

### Takeaway
The best measured evidence supports three things: **parallel (multiple simultaneous) prototyping beats serial iteration**, **examples and analogies help most when few, uncommon and at a moderate distance**, and **hypothesis-driven experimentation improves decisions under uncertainty** (RCTs with 116 and then 759 startups). TRIZ has small student-sample support for novelty and variety; design thinking rests mainly on case studies and surveys; set-based engineering, the Wright brothers, and Edison are case histories; the marshmallow challenge is anecdotal workshop data.

### Cited Findings

#### Prototyping strategy (strongest controlled evidence for "build to learn")
- **[Measured, small/student]** Dow, Glassco, Kass, Schwarz, Schwartz and Klemmer (2010, *ACM TOCHI*), "Parallel Prototyping Leads to Better Design Results, More Divergence, and Increased Self-Efficacy": participants designed web banner ads; the **parallel** group created several prototypes and got critique on the set, the **serial** group got critique after each one (both made about five prototypes plus a final). Parallel participants outperformed serial participants on all performance measures, including real click-through rates; independent raters judged parallel prototypes more diverse; parallel participants gained self-efficacy and serial participants did not — [UCSD ProtoLab page](https://protolab.ucsd.edu/?p=3561); [Stanford submitted PDF](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2010/parallel-prototyping/ParallelPrototyping2010-submitted.pdf); [Dow, CMU PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~spdow/files/PrototypingAll-theLink12.pdf).
- The ads ran as a real campaign for about 15 days with about 1 million ad appearances; users clicked parallel ads more per appearance and spent longer on the client site; expert raters (ad professionals, magazine editors) also scored parallel ads higher — [Dow, CMU PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~spdow/files/PrototypingAll-theLink12.pdf); [Cardinal Path summary](https://www.cardinalpath.com/web-design-the-case-for-parallel-prototyping/).
- **[Measured, small/student]** Replication in a physical domain: Murphy et al. (2022) ran a physical design competition and found clear advantages for parallel over iterative prototyping in design success and self-efficacy; Dow's own earlier physical-prototype study was largely inconclusive — [Murphy et al. 2022](https://edrl.engr.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/142/2022/02/Murphy2022_Article_ComparingParallelAndIterativeP.pdf); [Dow et al. 2009 tech report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~spdow/files/Prototyping-PSstudy-TechR09.pdf); [Dow, Heddleston & Klemmer 2009, "Efficacy of prototyping under time constraints"](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jianzhao/cs449-649/files/prototyping-iteration-dow09.pdf).
- A 2025 Cambridge study looked at first-year engineering students' self-directed use of parallel and iterative strategies — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5199FB635FF3E76F6C0B4DABDBBC9C2F/S2732527X25101739a.pdf/evaluation-of-self-directed-usage-of-parallel-and-iterative-prototyping-strategies-by-first-year-engineering-students.pdf).
- **[Measured, small/student]** Neeley, Lim, Zhu and Yang (MIT, ASME DTM 2013), "Building Fast to Think Faster": drawing on Kolb's experiential learning and an individual version of concurrent engineering, they propose an early process heavy in low-fidelity physical models and tested it with engineering students, using the number of prototypes required in the first iteration as the independent variable — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/120026).
- Camburn et al. (2017, *Design Science* 3:e13) reviewed prototyping literature across engineering, management, design science and architecture; they sort techniques into those that improve prototype outcomes directly and those that lower cost and time, and give a strategy matrix linking prototyping objectives to techniques — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/design-science/article/design-prototyping-methods-state-of-the-art-in-strategies-techniques-and-guidelines/560B306A5E799AEE54D30E0D2C1B7063).
- **[Anecdotal]** Marshmallow challenge (spaghetti tower; Tom Wujec and Peter Skillman): kindergarteners do better than the average of all groups tested and recent business-school graduates do worst. Wujec's explanation: business students spend most of the time planning and then execute once, leaving almost no time to fix the design when the marshmallow goes on top, while children start building straight away and keep redesigning. Skillman adds that adults spend effort on status ("who is in charge?"). Reported heights vary by source (e.g., ~10 in for MBAs vs ~26 in for kindergarteners), and the data are not peer-reviewed — [Tom Wujec](https://www.tomwujec.com/marshmallow-challenge); [Peter Skillman](https://medium.com/@peterskillman/the-design-challenge-also-called-spaghetti-tower-cda62685e15b); [TED transcript](https://opus.nlpl.eu/legacy/ParCor/Sentence_Aligned_Texts/TED/010_837.en.txt).

#### Analogy, examples and fixation
- **[Measured, small/student]** Jansson and Smith (1991, *Design Studies* 12(1):3-11) ran the first laboratory study of design fixation: designers shown a picture of an example solution (e.g., for a bicycle rack, a measuring cup for the blind, a spill-proof cup) repeated its features, even when told to avoid them; a fourth study showed the same effect in professional designers. It started what is now a sub-field — [Crilly, via core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/237398528.pdf); [Strathclyde: overcoming design fixation](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/id/eprint/79504); [UCL Discovery](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1318083/1/1318083.pdf).
- **[Measured — meta-analysis]** Sio, Kotovsky and Cagan (2015, *Design Studies* 39:70-99), 43 studies: examples make designers produce more example-related ideas and fewer categories of ideas (fixation), but the ideas are more novel, and quality correlates positively with the degree of copying. Benefits were greatest with **fewer and less common examples**; "one single and uncommon example" helped most, possibly by pushing deeper search in one remote domain — [British Library record](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100081707150_0x000001); [Design Studies record](https://ersearch2.cvtisr.sk/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c339t-226b9a1903a4e954576c8b615595421c6d32f1ad7ac2e0ce1f61d6fc827e625e3).
- **[Measured, small/student]** Chan, Fu, Schunn, Cagan, Wood and Kotovsky (2011, *Journal of Mechanical Design*): senior engineering students with patent examples in a 2x2 design (far vs near field, common vs less common) plus a no-example control. Far-field, less-common examples produced more novel concepts than control; the authors recommend far-field, less-common examples during ideation — [Chan et al. 2011 (Wisconsin copy)](https://edrl.engr.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/142/2022/02/2011-jmd-distancecommonness.pdf); [ICED 2011 version](https://www.designsociety.org/publication/30665/on_the_effective_use_of_design-by-analogy_the_influences_of_analogical_distance_and_commonness_of_analogous_designs_on_ideation_performance).
- Refinement and conflict: Fu et al. (2013, JMD) proposed a "sweet spot" of analogical distance between too near and too far; Chan and Schunn's later work disputes that more distant analogies are always better — [Fu et al. 2013](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/fuchanetal-near-far-2013.pdf); [Chan & Schunn](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/chan-schunn-analogy-concepts-2014.pdf); contradicted in part by [Chan et al. 2011](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/Chanetal2011.pdf).
- Biomimicry evidence is mostly case histories plus classroom protocol studies (Helms et al. 2009); a 2021 *Design Science* meta-analysis of biologically inspired design case studies exists (title: "Multifunctional and domain independent") but its findings were not retrieved — [Design Science 2021](https://www.cambridge.org:443/core/journals/design-science/article/multifunctional-and-domain-independent-a-metaanalysis-of-case-studies-of-biologically-inspired-design/154F3753DF4E95D9DFE0E7344E3D4542).

#### TRIZ
- **[Measured, small/student]** Vargas Hernandez, Schmidt and Okudan Kremer (2013, JMD, "Systematic ideation effectiveness study of TRIZ"), experiments at three institutions: TRIZ improved **novelty and variety** but slightly reduced **quantity**; the 2012 ASEE version concludes some ideation methods suit some tasks better depending on the outcome sought — [typeset.io PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/systematic-ideation-effectiveness-study-of-triz-2pddgdhms5.pdf); [ASEE 2012](https://peer.asee.org/21369).
- A 2023 ICED paper reports students produced 1.5 times more ideas with reformulated TRIZ principles than with classical methods, but the same author reports only a limited share of his case problems could be solved with TRIZ — [Cambridge Core, ICED 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-design-society/article/enhancing-engineering-creativity-with-automated-formulation-of-elementary-solution-principles/834EED7F55ABA5C0461C64F1CEFF13B6).
- Survey evidence: a questionnaire of 300+ Chinese engineers found economic and intellectual benefits, while other surveys it cites consider TRIZ's overall efficiency relatively low; a recent study pairing the contradiction matrix with LLM assistants found AI-generated solutions favour feasibility over novelty — [Cambridge Core study A](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/81CEAB2A6483D6A7D42010A22F5B9839/core-reader); [Cambridge Core study B](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/09B3955101E1013E73E8EB6C528DAE56/core-reader) (which claim sits in which paper was not verified from full text).
- Practitioner-project evidence exists (modern TRIZ in real consulting NPD projects) — [IFIP, hal-02905549](https://dl.ifip.org/hal-02905549v1); a practice review notes benefits remain prominent despite complexity and organisational barriers — [Ilevbare et al. 2013](https://typeset.io/papers/a-review-of-triz-and-its-benefits-and-challenges-in-practice-24hqatxxin).

#### Design thinking
- **[Case history]** Liedtka (2018, HBR) drew on a seven-year in-depth study of 50 projects across business, health care and social services — [O'Reilly / HBR](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/hbrs-10-must/9781633698819/ch02.html); [IDEO summary](https://designthinking.ideo.com/resources/why-design-thinking-works).
- Robbins and Fu (2022, *R&D Management*) state there is a "lack of a body of research that demonstrates systematically design thinking's ability to deliver value", quoting Liedtka's own 2020 work — [DCU DORAS record](https://doras.dcu.ie/26643/).
- Survey-based studies: Dell'Era et al. analysed 221 design-thinking consulting projects, and another study 146 projects by European consulting firms, looking at which practices correlate with project value — [Politecnico di Milano JKM 2024](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/1287883/1/10-1108_jkm-04-2024-0424.pdf); [Cambridge Core reader](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/2F6208B2CC9E175BCBC681B784404BDD/core-reader) (attribution of the 146-project study to this link is from search summary).
- Critiques **[Anecdotal / opinion]**: Natasha Jen (Pentagram, 2017 99U talk "Design Thinking is Bullsh*t") argues the hexagon-step version lacks the rigour of expert critique and proof; Lee Vinsel compares the five-step process to a freshman writing class. Defenders reply that Jen critiques a stereotype and also lacks evidence — [Print Magazine](https://www.printmag.com/design-criticism/design-thinking-process-bs/); [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/designers/product-design/design-thinking-criticism); [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/exploring-the-reasons-for-design-thinking-criticism-cf62eb765d60).

#### Scientific method / hypothesis-driven experimentation
- **[Measured — RCT]** Camuffo, Cordova, Gambardella and Spina (2020, *Management Science*): RCT with 116 Italian startups; founders trained in a scientific approach were more precise in decisions, more likely to drop bad projects and to pivot — [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/suppl/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3249); [INSEAD](https://www.insead.edu/faculty-research/publications/journal-articles/a-scientific-approach-entrepreneurial-decision-0); [Eller/Arizona paper](https://eller.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/Small%20changes%20with%20big%20impact-%20experimental%20evidence%20of%20a%20scientific%20approach%20to%20the%20decision-making%20of%20entrepreneurial%20firms.pdf).
- **[Measured — RCT replication]** Large-scale replication (4 RCTs, 759 firms; *Strategic Management Journal*, CEPR DP15972): treated founders made **one or two major strategic shifts** and were less likely to never pivot or to pivot endlessly; fewer pivots associated with higher performance. Among the top 25% of revenue earners, scientific-approach startups earned about €28,000 more than controls over the experiment (INSEAD reports €492,000 for the top 5%) — [CEPR DP15972](https://cepr.org/publications/dp15972); [INSEAD Knowledge](https://knowledge.insead.edu/entrepreneurship/science-successful-start-ups); [SDA Bocconi](https://www.sdabocconi.it/en/sda-bocconi-insight/knowledge/the-science-of-navigating-business-uncertainty/).
- Related: Valentine et al. (2024), "The theory-based view and strategic pivots: the effects of theorization and experimentation" separates theorising from experimenting — [City St George's open access record](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/34139/).

#### Set-based concurrent engineering
- **[Case history]** Evidence comes from Toyota case study (Sobek, Ward & Liker 1999; Ward et al. 1995) and a US-Japan supplier study (Liker, Sobek, Ward & Cristiano 1996, *IEEE Trans. Eng. Mgmt.*) — [Sloan Management Review](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/toyotas-principles-of-setbased-concurrent-engineering); [Toche et al. 2020 review](https://publications.polymtl.ca/9280/1/2020_Toche_Set-based_design_review_new_directions.pdf).

#### Research through design and co-evolution
- **[Measured, small — protocol study]** Dorst and Cross (2001, *Design Studies* 22(5):425-437): protocol studies of nine experienced industrial designers; creative design involves the **co-evolution** of problem and solution spaces, with designers working on both at the same time; introduces "default" and "surprise" problem/solution spaces. A 2026 DRS scoping review calls the model highly influential — [Open University ORO](https://oro.open.ac.uk/3278); [DRS 2026 scoping review](https://dl.designresearchsociety.org/drs-conference-papers/drs2026/researchpapers/240).

#### Wright brothers and Edison (case histories)
- Wright brothers: lift shortfall to one-third of prediction; bicycle rig; wind tunnel Sept-Dec 1901 with 100-200 models; 1902 glider used the tunnel data and solved the 1900-1901 problems; 300+ glides; first three-axis active control — [NASA Glenn](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/K-12/airplane/wrights/results.html); [NASA Glenn test 1901](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/WWW/K-12/airplane/wrights/test1901.html); [NASA Glenn unpowered aircraft](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/unpowered-aircraft).
- Edison: organised lab of ~60, systematic material screening, development through to a 1,200-lamp-per-day factory — [Edison Papers](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biographical-essays/lighting/from-research-to-development).

### Inferences
- The measured evidence base is strongest for the **process behaviours** shared across methods (parallel exploration, cheap prototypes, explicit hypotheses, few uncommon examples) rather than for any branded method as a whole.
- The Dow result plus the Camuffo result jointly suggest: build several distinct variants at once, compare them side by side against explicit criteria, and commit to a small number of deliberate changes in direction rather than drifting.
- For a movement system, the fixation evidence warns that copying a reference game's controller will anchor ideas; the Sio meta-analysis suggests that one deliberately uncommon reference (e.g., an animal gait, a sport technique) is more useful than a stack of similar games.
- Marshmallow-challenge evidence is weak in rigour but consistent in direction with the controlled parallel-prototyping studies: early physical testing exposes the "hidden assumption" (the marshmallow's weight) that analysis misses.

### Gaps
- Participant counts and effect sizes for Dow et al. 2010 could not be verified (a "33 participants" figure was not confirmed); the outcome of Neeley et al. 2013 was not retrieved.
- No controlled study comparing whole methods head to head (e.g., TRIZ vs. design thinking vs. set-based) was found.
- No quantitative performance data for Toyota set-based development (time, cost) were retrieved in this session.
- The Design Science 2021 biomimicry meta-analysis findings, and controlled studies of biomimicry versus other ideation methods, were not retrieved.
- No study was found measuring morphological analysis outcomes against a control.

## 3. How do the methods compare: when to use which, how they combine, and common pitfalls?

### Takeaway
Use framing tools (first principles, TRIZ, morphological charts) when the problem is understood but no solution exists; use analogy when you need new mechanisms; use design thinking or RtD when the problem itself is unclear or human-centred; use set-based engineering and parallel prototyping when several subsystems must fit together and early commitment is risky; use the Wright/scientific loop when the system is dynamic and must be measured to be understood. The main shared pitfalls are fixation on early examples, premature convergence on one point design, testing only the whole system, and over-engineering before a cheap probe.

### Cited Findings

#### Fit by problem type (from the documented scope of each method)
| Method | Best suited for | Evidence tag | Key weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| First principles | Challenging inherited assumptions, cost or physics floors | Anecdotal | Decomposition can miss system-level interactions; no controlled evidence |
| Analogy / biomimicry | Finding new mechanisms for a well-defined function | Measured (student) + case histories | Fixation, surface-level copying, misapplied analogies |
| TRIZ | Technical problems expressible as contradictions | Measured (student), surveys | Complex to learn; no rule for choosing tools; limited share of problems solvable |
| Design thinking / Double Diamond | Unclear, human-centred problems needing reframing | Case studies, surveys | Weak systematic evidence; can become a ritual of steps |
| Scientific / hypothesis loop | High uncertainty decisions; deciding what to keep | RCTs (startups) | Needs measurable hypotheses; slow if each test is expensive |
| Research through design | Novel interactive artifacts where making reveals the problem | Accepted in HCI; little outcome data | Hard to evaluate; risk of "making without learning" |
| Morphological analysis | Multi-dimensional, non-quantifiable problems | Practitioner reports | Combinatorial explosion; needs consistency screening |
| Set-based engineering | Many interacting subsystems, costly late changes | Case history (Toyota) | Requires discipline and parallel effort up front |
| Wright approach | Dynamic, controlled systems where whole tests are uninformative | Case history | Needs ability to build single-function test rigs |
| Build-measure-learn | Unknown user response; fast cheap iterations possible | RCTs (scientific approach) | Can degenerate into endless pivots without theory |

Sources for the table rows: first principles — [Quartz](https://qz.com/972118/simple-math-is-why-elon-musks-companies-keep-doing-what-others-dont-even-consider-possible/); analogy — [Chan et al. 2011](https://edrl.engr.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/142/2022/02/2011-jmd-distancecommonness.pdf), [AI EDAM](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ai-edam/article/design-problem-solving-with-biological-analogies-a-verbal-protocol-study/F69BBEE77460DA27C40972AD03C10C21); TRIZ — [Hernandez et al. 2013](https://typeset.io/pdf/systematic-ideation-effectiveness-study-of-triz-2pddgdhms5.pdf), [Ilevbare et al. 2013](https://typeset.io/papers/a-review-of-triz-and-its-benefits-and-challenges-in-practice-24hqatxxin), [ICED 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-design-society/article/enhancing-engineering-creativity-with-automated-formulation-of-elementary-solution-principles/834EED7F55ABA5C0461C64F1CEFF13B6); design thinking — [Robbins & Fu 2022](https://doras.dcu.ie/26643/), [Print](https://www.printmag.com/design-criticism/design-thinking-process-bs/); scientific loop — [CEPR](https://cepr.org/publications/dp15972); RtD — [Zimmerman et al. 2007](https://courses.ischool.berkeley.edu/i262/s13/readings_pdf/Zimmerman_Research_Through_Design_as_a_Method_for_IaD_in_HCI_0.pdf); morphological — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphological_analysis_(problem-solving)), [Ritchey](https://ia600207.us.archive.org/27/items/ritchey_swemorph_Gma/gma.pdf); set-based — [Toche et al. 2020](https://publications.polymtl.ca/9280/1/2020_Toche_Set-based_design_review_new_directions.pdf); Wright — [Bradshaw](https://invention.psychology.msstate.edu/Tale_of_Airplane/Design.html); build-measure-learn — [INSEAD](https://knowledge.insead.edu/entrepreneurship/science-successful-start-ups). The "best suited" and "weakness" cells are a synthesis of those sources (see Inferences).

#### Documented pitfalls
- **Fixation**: examples cause copying of their features, even when designers are told to avoid them, and even in professionals — [Jansson & Smith 1991 via Crilly](https://core.ac.uk/download/237398528.pdf); examples narrow the number of idea categories — [Sio et al. 2015](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100081707150_0x000001). Solution fixation is also one of the recurring errors in biomimetic design — [AI EDAM](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ai-edam/article/design-problem-solving-with-biological-analogies-a-verbal-protocol-study/F69BBEE77460DA27C40972AD03C10C21).
- **Premature convergence / single point design**: set-based engineering exists precisely to avoid repeatedly criticising and changing one option; Toyota delays commitment while sets are narrowed — [Toche et al. 2020](https://publications.polymtl.ca/9280/1/2020_Toche_Set-based_design_review_new_directions.pdf); [Ward et al. 1995](https://www.usmcu.edu/Portals/218/HD/Digitized%20Collections/COLL_2062%20John%20Boyd/COLL%202062_John%20Boyd_Box%2011/110305-The%20Second%20Toyota%20Paradox%20How%20Delaying%20Decisions%20Can%20Make%20Better%20Cars%20Faster%20Sloan%20Management%20Review%20Spring%201995%20with%20attached%20Note.PDF). Serial prototyping produced less diverse and lower-performing results than parallel — [Dow et al. 2010](https://protolab.ucsd.edu/?p=3561).
- **Plan-then-build-once (over-engineering before probing)**: the marshmallow-challenge pattern where planners run out of time to fix the design — [Tom Wujec](https://www.tomwujec.com/marshmallow-challenge).
- **Whole-system testing only**: global performance from field trials does not tell the inventor which feature to improve — [Bradshaw](https://invention.psychology.msstate.edu/Tale_of_Airplane/Design.html).
- **Trusting inherited data**: the Wrights' early gliders were designed with tables that did not transfer to their configuration (aspect ratio, Smeaton coefficient); they caught it only by measuring — [NASA Glenn](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/K-12/airplane/wrights/results.html); [Centennial of Flight](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080917114018/http://centennialofflight.gov/essay/Wright_Bros/1901/WR3.htm).
- **Endless pivoting or no pivoting**: the untreated startups were more likely to never pivot or pivot endlessly — [INSEAD Knowledge](https://knowledge.insead.edu/entrepreneurship/science-successful-start-ups).
- **Too-far analogies**: some evidence that very distant analogies stop helping; a "sweet spot" is proposed — [Fu et al. 2013](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/fuchanetal-near-far-2013.pdf).
- **Method as ritual**: design thinking criticised when reduced to a step diagram without critique or proof — [Print Magazine](https://www.printmag.com/design-criticism/design-thinking-process-bs/).
- **Method complexity**: TRIZ's complexity and lack of tool-selection rules hinder adoption — [Ilevbare et al. 2013](https://typeset.io/papers/a-review-of-triz-and-its-benefits-and-challenges-in-practice-24hqatxxin).

#### How they combine (documented combinations)
- Analogy within TRIZ: TRIZ itself is an analogy engine over the patent corpus (similar contradictions, similar solutions) — [WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/export/sites/www/tisc/en/docs/tisc-toolkit-triz-description.pdf).
- The Shinkansen case combined analogy (kingfisher) with systematic scale-model experiments — [Trellis](https://trellis.net/article/how-one-engineers-birdwatching-made-japans-bullet-train-better).
- The Wrights combined theory-driven reasoning (reducing search), functional decomposition, purpose-built measurement rigs, and iterative full-scale gliders — [Bradshaw lecture](https://www-alg.ist.hokudai.ac.jp/~thomas/ALT05/gary.html); [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/roadtofirstflight.htm).
- Neeley et al. explicitly combine Kolb's learning cycle with an individual-scale version of concurrent (set-based) engineering — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/120026).
- The Double Diamond embeds diverge-converge twice (problem space, then solution space) and its Deliver stage is small-scale test-and-reject — [Design Council](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/framework-for-innovation).

### Inferences
- A practical combination for an unsolved physical/interactive system: (1) first-principles and functional decomposition to name the subproblems and hard limits; (2) TRIZ-style contradiction statements for the hardest trade-offs; (3) a morphological chart per subproblem filled partly from one or two uncommon analogies (biology, sport, robotics); (4) set-based narrowing with several variants kept alive per subsystem; (5) Wright-style single-function rigs with numeric measurements; (6) parallel prototypes compared side by side; (7) hypothesis-driven decisions with a limit on major pivots.
- For a game character's movement, the "whole-system test only" pitfall is the most likely: tuning everything inside the full game makes feel problems impossible to localise. Single-function test pages with readouts (the equivalent of the Wrights' balances) counter this.
- Over-engineering risk is high for movement systems because physics-based approaches invite building a full simulation first; the marshmallow and parallel-prototyping evidence argues for crude placeholder versions of several approaches before investing in one.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found that tests a specific multi-method combination (e.g., TRIZ + set-based) against single methods.
- No source was retrieved that directly applies these invention methods to character locomotion or game movement design; the transfer points are inferences.

## 4. What do the methods have in common — is there a consensus "core loop" for inventing under uncertainty?

### Takeaway
Yes, broadly. Across design research, engineering history and the entrepreneurship RCTs, the shared loop is: **frame the problem (and expect to reframe it) → diverge to many candidate solutions or sub-solutions → make cheap, concrete probes → test them against explicit, measurable criteria, ideally one function at a time → reflect, update the framing, and converge deliberately → repeat at higher fidelity.** Problem and solution evolve together; the loop is iterative, not linear.

### Cited Findings
- **Diverge-converge**: the Double Diamond (from Banathy's 1996 divergence-convergence model) alternates widening and narrowing twice and is explicitly non-linear — [Design Council](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/framework-for-innovation); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Diamond_(design_process_model)).
- **Problem and solution co-evolve**: experienced designers develop the problem and solution together; creative steps occur when a "surprise" in one space reframes the other — [Dorst & Cross 2001](https://oro.open.ac.uk/3278); [DRS 2026 review](https://dl.designresearchsociety.org/drs-conference-papers/drs2026/researchpapers/240).
- **Making is a form of thinking**: designs are rarely fully formed in advance; designers learn from the medium's backtalk — [Schön interview](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/bds/9-schon.html); [Schön 1992](https://www.uio.no/studier/emner/matnat/ifi/IN1060/v25/pensum/schon_design-conversation.pdf). RtD treats the artifact as the vehicle of inquiry — [Zimmerman et al. 2007](https://courses.ischool.berkeley.edu/i262/s13/readings_pdf/Zimmerman_Research_Through_Design_as_a_Method_for_IaD_in_HCI_0.pdf).
- **Invention as search, made efficient by theory and decomposition**: invention is heuristic search through a design space where good solutions are rare; searching function by function and using theory reduces the search — [Bradshaw (Springer)](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/11563983_1.pdf); [Cognitive Science Society article](https://journalpub.escholarship.org/cognitivesciencesociety/article/32905/galley/23965/download); [Bradshaw lecture](https://www-alg.ist.hokudai.ac.jp/~thomas/ALT05/gary.html).
- **Keep several options alive, then narrow**: set-based engineering maps the space and narrows by intersecting feasible sets — [Toche et al. 2020](https://publications.polymtl.ca/9280/1/2020_Toche_Set-based_design_review_new_directions.pdf); parallel prototyping beats serial — [Dow et al. 2010](https://protolab.ucsd.edu/?p=3561); [Murphy et al. 2022](https://edrl.engr.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/142/2022/02/Murphy2022_Article_ComparingParallelAndIterativeP.pdf).
- **Learn by experiment, with theory**: hypothesis-driven founders made fewer, more deliberate pivots and performed better — [CEPR DP15972](https://cepr.org/publications/dp15972); the Wrights replaced trust in published data with their own measurements — [NASA Glenn](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/K-12/airplane/wrights/results.html).
- **Experiential learning cycle**: Neeley et al. ground rapid early prototyping in Kolb's theory of experiential learning — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/120026).
- **Cheap probes first**: prototyping techniques are split between those that improve outcomes and those that cut cost/time, implying both matter — [Camburn et al. 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/design-science/article/design-prototyping-methods-state-of-the-art-in-strategies-techniques-and-guidelines/560B306A5E799AEE54D30E0D2C1B7063); children's build-early approach beats plan-first in the marshmallow challenge — [Tom Wujec](https://www.tomwujec.com/marshmallow-challenge).
- Note: the 1995 "Second Toyota Paradox" article survives in the papers of John Boyd (originator of the OODA observe-orient-decide-act loop), showing the link between set-based thinking and loop-based decision models was noticed at the time — [USMCU Boyd collection](https://www.usmcu.edu/Portals/218/HD/Digitized%20Collections/COLL_2062%20John%20Boyd/COLL%202062_John%20Boyd_Box%2011/110305-The%20Second%20Toyota%20Paradox%20How%20Delaying%20Decisions%20Can%20Make%20Better%20Cars%20Faster%20Sloan%20Management%20Review%20Spring%201995%20with%20attached%20Note.PDF).

### Inferences
- A consensus core loop for inventing under uncertainty, synthesised from the sources above:
  1. **Frame**: state the goal as functions and measurable criteria, not as a form; name the critical unsolved subproblem (the Wrights' "control").
  2. **Decompose**: split into subfunctions; find hard limits from first principles; write the key trade-offs as contradictions.
  3. **Diverge**: generate a set of options per subfunction (morphological chart), seeded by one or two uncommon analogies; avoid anchoring on a single reference.
  4. **Probe cheaply and in parallel**: build crude, single-function rigs for several options at once.
  5. **Measure**: test each rig against explicit numeric or observable criteria, one function at a time, plus a human judgement pass for qualities numbers miss.
  6. **Reflect and reframe**: update the problem statement from surprises (co-evolution); record what was learned.
  7. **Converge deliberately**: eliminate options that fail, intersect feasible sets across subsystems, and make a small number of major direction changes rather than constant drift.
  8. **Integrate and repeat** at higher fidelity, re-testing earlier functions when new ones are added.
- Transfer to a game character movement system: the loop maps onto building separate test pages per movement function (balance, foot placement, turning, acceleration, input mapping), each with numeric readouts, running two or three competing approaches side by side, and only integrating a function into the full character once it passes its own test. The human "feel" judgement remains the equivalent of the Wrights' test flights: necessary, but used after component tests, not instead of them.
- The evidence suggests the loop's power comes from three habits more than from any named method: **parallelism** (several options at once), **isolation** (testing one function at a time), and **explicit hypotheses** (knowing in advance what result would change the plan).

### Gaps
- No single authoritative source states a "consensus core loop"; the eight-step loop above is a synthesis across the cited sources, not a published standard.
- NASA or other engineering design handbooks describing the formal engineering design process were not retrieved in this session.
- No empirical study tests the full combined loop against a control.
