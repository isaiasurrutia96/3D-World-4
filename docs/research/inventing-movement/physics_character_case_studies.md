## Key Question 1: NaturalMotion Euphoria / Dynamic Motion Synthesis. How did Torsten Reil's evolutionary robotics research become Euphoria, and what is publicly known about how it works?

### Takeaway
Euphoria began as an academic attempt to understand biological motor control: Reil used genetic algorithms to evolve neural-network "nervous systems" that made a physically simulated biped walk. That became an offline stunt tool (Endorphin, used in films such as *Troy*) and then a real-time game runtime (Euphoria in GTA IV, Red Dead Redemption, Backbreaker and others). Publicly, Euphoria is described as a rigid-body simulation plus "AI controller modules" (adaptive behaviours) that drive simulated muscles. In shipped games it mostly took over for a few seconds during hits and falls and then handed control back to motion capture. It did not replace locomotion animation.

### Cited Findings
**Academic origin (the "starting idea" and what was borrowed)**
- Reil started as a DPhil student in Oxford's Zoology Department. He built computer simulations of nervous systems to understand how they control movement, using genetic-algorithm programs in which the simulated characters evolved their own ways of moving — [Oxford Biology: "NaturalMotion: from neural research to Grand Theft Auto"](https://www.biology.ox.ac.uk/node/2151801) (via search summary)
- The method as reported: generate 100 characters, each with a randomly assembled neural network controlling its muscles; repeatedly breed the best performers with mutations; this was eventually cut down to about 20 generations and a few minutes of computation — [Times Higher Education, "Findings: Step forward for animation"](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/36671)
- Peer-reviewed core: Reil & Husbands, "Evolution of Central Pattern Generators for Bipedal Walking in a Real-Time Physics Environment," *IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation* 6(2), April 2002. They evolved recurrent neural networks (central pattern generators, CPGs) to control a biped in a full rigid-body physics simulation. The result was stable straight-line walking on flat ground with **no proprioceptive input** (no sensing of the body's own joint state). A simple sound-localisation input was added for steering. The repository record calls it the first successful use of evolutionary robotics for realistic 3D bipedal walking — [Reil & Husbands IEEE PDF](https://users.sussex.ac.uk/~philh/pubs/reilhusbandsIEEE.pdf); [Sussex figshare record](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Evolution_of_Central_pattern_Generators_for_Bipedal_Walking_in_a_Real-time_Physics_Environment/23352452)
- Later papers describe the setup as a genetic algorithm optimising fully connected recurrent networks used as CPGs, with real-valued encoding of weights, time constants and biases — [Wolff et al. 2006](https://www.am.chalmers.se/~wolff/AI2/SMC2006_KW.JP.AH.MW.pdf)
- Fields borrowed from: zoology/neuroscience (CPGs, motor control), evolutionary computation (genetic algorithms), evolutionary robotics (co-author Husbands, Sussex cognitive science), and rigid-body physics simulation — [Reil & Husbands IEEE PDF](https://users.sussex.ac.uk/~philh/pubs/reilhusbandsIEEE.pdf)

**From lab to company**
- Isis Innovation (Oxford's tech-transfer office) funded Reil's work with Colm Massey on a prototype in 2000 — [Times Higher Education, "Student's stuntmen join march into Troy"](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/43028)
- NaturalMotion was founded in November 2001 to commercialise "Dynamic Motion Synthesis (DMS)" — [PSU interview with NaturalMotion](https://www.psu.com/news/psu-interviews-naturalmotion/). Founders are given as Reil, Colm Massey and David Raubenheimer, with help from Oxford University Innovation — [Oxford University Innovation](https://innovation.ox.ac.uk/?p=9022)
- Reil has said he founded the company while trying to finish his PhD, had to choose one, and chose NaturalMotion — [PocketGamer.biz Hall of Fame](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/hall-of-fame-torsten-reil/)
- First product: **Endorphin**, an offline tool for "virtual stuntmen" built on DMS, whose results were baked to animation for playback. Endorphin 1.0 launched at SIGGRAPH 2003. Endorphin 2.6 added an "adaptive behaviors" feature — [PSU interview p.3](https://www.psu.com/news/psu-interviews-naturalmotion/3/); [Wikipedia: NaturalMotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NaturalMotion)
- First contract: Moving Picture Company, for battle scenes in *Troy*. MPC's Julian Mann joked that Endorphin stuntmen "never sleep" and keep working after being decapitated — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/43028)
- Reil gave a TED talk titled "Using Biology to Make Better Animation" — [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/ted_talk_reil/) (content not retrieved)

**Euphoria (real-time) and what is public about how it works**
- NaturalMotion's own description: Euphoria is "not based on canned data recall, but on fast simulations of the game characters' actual motor control, muscles and biomechanics." Its core is "a small kernel, AI controller modules (for adaptive behaviours) and a rigid dynamics module (for physics)" — [AWN press release](https://www.awn.com/news/naturalmotion-announces-euphoria-next-gen-game-platforms); [GTAForums repost of 2007 NaturalMotion/Rockstar announcement](https://gtaforums.com/topic/268295-naturalmotion-partners-with-rockstar-official-news-022707/) (press-release language)
- Euphoria is DMS running in real time: it simulates biomechanics and the motor nervous system and synthesises actions and reactions at runtime instead of playing back clips — [Wikipedia: Euphoria (software)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria_(software))
- GTA IV (2008, Rockstar's RAGE engine) was Rockstar's first Euphoria game — [Game Developer product note](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/product-i-grand-theft-auto-iv-i-using-naturalmotion-s-euphoria); [Wikipedia: Euphoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria_(software))
- AiGameDev's critical editorial: in GTA IV, Euphoria's typical role was an improved ragdoll that took control for a few seconds and then blended back to motion capture. Most locomotion was animation blending with weights chosen at runtime — [AiGameDev, "NaturalMotion's euphoria Technology: The Honeymoon is Over"](http://aigamedev.com/open/editorial/naturalmotion-euphoria/)
- **Backbreaker** (NaturalMotion's own first game; announced August 2007, delayed to mid-2010): tackles were simulated on the fly rather than keyframed or motion-captured, marketed as "no two tackles are the same." Reviewers praised the animation system while criticising other parts of the game — [Wikipedia: Backbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backbreaker_(video_game)). Backbreaker ran Euphoria on top of NVIDIA PhysX — [MCV: NaturalMotion uses PhysX in Backbreaker](https://www.mcvuk.com/development/naturalmotion-uses-physx-in-backbreaker)
- Behaviour names visible through GTA V modding include BodyBalance, StaggerFall, FallToKnees, ragdoll "relax" and NM "agents." Modders say StaggerFall avoids stiffness glitches that FallToKnees had, and that BodyBalance can be raised for armoured peds so they stay up longer when shot — [RAGEuphoria mod](https://www.gta5-mods.com/misc/rageuphoria); [NaturalMotion Euphoria Mod changelog](https://www.gta5-mods.com/tr/misc/naturalmotion-euphoria-oiv-kaai210-bravercoolio-jedijosh920); [KoreaBank's Euphoria](https://cs.gta5-mods.com/misc/koreabank-s-euphoria-kbe) (community sources, not official documentation)
- A Google Patents result for US7685081B2, "Bipedal walking simulation," came up in searches about Reil's work — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7685081). I could not open it to confirm the assignee or the claims.
- In 2017 NaturalMotion (by then owned by Zynga) stopped licensing Euphoria and its other technology to focus on mobile games — [Wikipedia: Euphoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria_(software))
- Reil is now co-founder and co-CEO of the defence company Helsing. A 2025 German article says Euphoria was used in GTA IV and V and is in GTA VI — [Abendzeitung München](https://www.abendzeitung-muenchen.de/muenchen/gta-6-was-der-muenchner-ruestungskonzern-helsing-damit-zu-tun-hat-art-1174480). The GTA VI claim is a press assertion, not confirmed by Rockstar in anything I found.

### Inferences
- (Inference) The path was: understand biology, then simulate it (evolved CPGs), then use it offline where failures can be discarded (film stunts in Endorphin), then use it in real time in short, bounded windows (hit and fall reactions that blend back to mocap). Each step lowered the reliability the controller needed. A controller that only has to look good for about two seconds after a hit is far easier to ship than one that has to walk all game.
- (Inference) The 2002 walkers needed no proprioception, so they walked open-loop on flat ground only. Game behaviours such as BodyBalance and StaggerFall must react to pushes and terrain, so they would need feedback (sensing the centre of mass, foot contacts and so on). That suggests the commercial runtime relied on engineered, sensor-driven controllers ("AI controller modules") more than on raw evolved networks. Some secondary sources say Euphoria uses "a form of neural networks" as controllers. I found no primary NaturalMotion statement confirming how much of shipped Euphoria was evolved and how much was hand-engineered.

### Gaps
- No primary NaturalMotion technical paper or GDC talk on Euphoria's controller design (joint motor model, balance strategy, behaviour blending) was found. The "Euphoria 3D White Paper DMS" on [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/doc/36401342/Euphoria-3D-White-Paper-Dms) exists but could not be opened.
- The Oxford Biology history page and Reil's TED talk could not be fetched, so direct Reil quotes about what failed (for example, why pure evolution did not scale to game behaviours) are missing.
- Unverified, from memory and not found in sources: that NaturalMotion's later controllers were mostly hand-designed by engineers, with evolution and optimisation used only for tuning. Do not state this as fact without a source.
- Red Dead Redemption and Star Wars: The Force Unleashed used Euphoria according to general knowledge and Wikipedia, but I found no developer-level detail on how they tuned it.

## Key Question 2: How were the indie and mid-size physics-character games built and iterated (Gang Beasts, Human Fall Flat, TABS, QWOP and Getting Over It, Toribash, Exanima, Stair/Turbo Dismount, Sumotori Dreams, Fall Guys, Party Animals, Rain World, plus Baby Steps and Half Sword)?

### Takeaway
Almost every one of these started as a small character-controller experiment, often with no animator, and the game was found afterwards. Developers built a physics body first, gave it a few simple "muscles" or balance forces, played with it until something was funny or satisfying, and then designed levels, rules and even the character's look around what the physics could do well. Detailed implementation write-ups are rare. Landfall (a balance script that pushes the feet under the centre of mass) and Toribash (18 one-axis joints with four states) are the most concrete public descriptions.

### Cited Findings
**Gang Beasts (Boneloaf: brothers James, Michael and Jonathan Brown)**
- Boneloaf formed in 2011 and made many game-jam prototypes on "variations of a custom physics manipulating character pose and movement system" — [Giant Bomb: Boneloaf](https://Www.giantbomb.com/boneloaf/3010-9617/)
- Origin: they reused the physics characters from their earlier *Grim Beasts* project because "we didn't have an animator" (James Brown), and added a simple punch: "They were more slapping than punching, but it was still somewhat satisfying" — [GamesRadar, "Funny how..."](https://gamesradar.com/funny-how-changing-face-comedy-modern-video-games)
- Michael Brown: physics drove the look. "In order to get the physics functioning in the way it needed to, it dictated even the game's iconic character design." They "messed around with the physics, and just slowly evolved the character from that," and have built "so many different variations of physics characters" — [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/?p=215072)
- Brown called character movement physics the hardest feature to build, because the game has no preset animation sequences — [Unity case study: Gang Beasts](https://unity3d.com/case-study/gang-beasts)
- Early coverage described "jelly bean-like characters" — [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/introducing-gang-beasts/). A 2014 bit-tech interview caught the game in pre-alpha — [bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/pc/interview-gang-beasts/1/). A 2015 GDC Vault video is a demo, not a technical talk — [Game Developer video post](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-multiplayer-brawling-with-i-gang-beasts-i-)
- Attribution warning: an itch.io comment says "a mix of techniques from a talk about Overgrowth and research into IK & PID controllers... applied to fully physics based ragdoll... custom controllers which has some PID similarities but simulates muscles instead of a motor" — [itch.io post 11835](https://itch.io/post/11835). One search summary attributed this to Boneloaf, but a second search showed it is an unnamed itch.io developer talking about their own game. **Do not attribute it to Boneloaf.** It is still a useful example of how indie developers combine Rosen's GDC talk with PID-style controllers.

**Human: Fall Flat (Tomas Sakalauskas, No Brakes Games, Lithuania; released 2016)**
- Origin: "a complete accident, an experiment to create a new kind of character controller," first built for Intel's RealSense hand-tracking camera. He switched to a gamepad build to prototype faster — [PC Games Insider (first-person piece)](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/success-story/66739/human-fall-flat-shows-that-releasing-a-game-is-just-a-lottery-anyone-can-play-but-you-cant-plan-on-winning/); [GamingBolt interview](https://gamingbolt.com/human-fall-flat-interview-what-dreams-may-come)
- Character before content: he began "prototyping physics-based controls, trying to have independent control of each arm. Only when I got the character more or less right, I started building initial puzzles" — [TheGamer interview](https://www.thegamer.com/human-fall-flat-interview-tomas-sakalauskas/)
- What failed: separate sticks or D-pads per arm "could work" but would be "really messy controls," so the controls were reworked. The shipped game maps each arm to a trigger (L2/R2 grab with that hand) and aims with the camera — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/human-fall-flat-interview-tomas-sakalauskas/) / [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/human-fall-flat-interview-what-dreams-may-come) (search summary did not say which of the two); [Push Square review](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps4/human_fall_flat)
- The plan shifted from "Portal meets Limbo" because playtesters, including his son, ignored the puzzles and played with the physics. Puzzles were deliberately "not really watertight" — [Wikipedia: Human: Fall Flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human:_Fall_Flat) (secondary)
- Engine workaround: "if the colliders are not working, then you place extra colliders that are hidden just to help support the weight of the objects" — reported from his interviews ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/human-fall-flat-interview-tomas-sakalauskas/) / [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/human-fall-flat-interview-what-dreams-may-come))
- No Brakes' own pitch: "direct and complete control of the character, nothing is scripted, no limits imposed" — [No Brakes itch.io page](https://nobrakesgames.itch.io/human)
- Key tuning lesson (2026 retrospective): Human Fall Flat 2 went down the "wrong branch of physics." It was "too stiff. Too polished," lost the clumsiness that made the original endearing, and was cancelled in favour of Human Fall Flat 3 — [Game Developer: "Human Fall Flat 2 is cancelled..."](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/-human-fall-flat-2-is-cancelled-we-are-making-human-fall-flat-3-no-brakes-games-founder-looks-back-on-a-defining-decade)

**Totally Accurate Battle Simulator, Stick Fight, Clustertruck (Landfall Games, Sweden)**
- Wilhelm Nylund's balance script: "The balance script adds force to the legs and feet to try to keep them in position to help support the body. For example, if both feet are in front of the body's center of mass one of them needs to move backwards so that the character can stay balanced" — [Game Developer: "How Landfall Games finds the fun in physics engines" (2018)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-landfall-games-finds-the-fun-in-physics-engines)
- Architecture change: the older physics-animation system bundled "keep upright," ground checks and walk-cycle leg rotation into one file. The newer one splits these into separate component scripts, similar to what they used for Stick Fight — [same article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-landfall-games-finds-the-fun-in-physics-engines)
- Process: rapid prototyping, reusing one humanoid model across many unit types, and "not taking the game too seriously." Every project needs a new physics-based control scheme that feels responsive and fun while leaving room for silly emergent humour (Petter Henriksson) — [same article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-landfall-games-finds-the-fun-in-physics-engines)
- Clustertruck came from finding that staying on top of moving physics objects was fun; the trucks were chosen early — [Game Developer blog: How TABS creator Landfall got discovery right](https://gamedeveloper.com/blogs/how-i-tabs-i-creator-landfall-got-discovery-right-)
- Nylund spoke at GDC 2020 on Landfall's physics approach and gave a talk titled "Unconventional Ways of Using Physics in Game Development" — [Game Developer: GDC 2020 preview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/learn-to-physics-up-your-game-the-landfall-way-at-gdc-2020-); [This Aarhus](https://thisaarhus.com/speaker/unconventional-ways-of-using-physics-in-game-development/)
- Landfall Archives includes an "interactive slideshow made to be used as a presentation explaining the principles of the physics used in Landfall's games" — [MobyGames: Landfall Archives](https://www.mobygames.com/game/201331)
- TABS is built in Unity; Early Access in April 2019, full release April 2021 — [Wikipedia: TABS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_Accurate_Battle_Simulator)

**QWOP, GIRP, Getting Over It, Baby Steps (Bennett Foddy and collaborators)**
- QWOP was made "almost as a gag, or very quickly anyway, in a week or two in 2008," while Foddy was a philosophy postdoc in the US, using Box2D — [PC Gamer interview](https://pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/bennett-foddy-designer-of-qwop-and-baby-steps-is-obsessed-with-friendslop-games-and-wont-uninstall-baldurs-gate-3-even-though-hell-probably-never-finish-it-it-was-too-big-and-so-i-stopped/); [Software Engineering Daily transcript (2026)](https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/SED1909-Bennett-Foddy.txt)
- Control mapping: Q and W drive the thighs, O and P the calves, so the player is the motor controller — [Wikipedia: QWOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QWOP). Coverage frames the near-impossibility as deliberate comedy built on failure — [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/qwop-turned-failure-into-comedy-and-found-viral-immorta-1826394272)
- Getting Over It (2017) is an homage to Jazzuo's *Sexy Hiking*. Unlike Sexy Hiking, it uses a real physics engine because Foddy wanted "heavy, high-energy physics interactions" — [Game Developer: Road to the IGF, Getting Over It](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-bennett-foddy-s-i-getting-over-it-i-); [PC Gamer: Foddy and Derek Yu on Sexy Hiking and B-games](https://pcgamer.com/the-creators-of-spelunky-and-getting-over-it-with-bennett-foddy-on-sexy-hiking-and-b-games)
- Felt behaviour: the mouse is not mapped 1:1 to the hammer and there is a slight lag, so players over- or under-commit. One input supports pushing, pulling, flinging, swinging and pogoing — [critpoints review](https://critpoints.net/2020/12/13/getting-over-it-with-bennet-foddy-review) (critic analysis)
- **Baby Steps** (Gabe Cuzzillo, Maxi Boch, Foddy): Cuzzillo said figuring out the walking was "by far the biggest technical challenge," and it took many years of iteration to get stable, tight controls while keeping the physics stable and the motion semi-natural. Feet first lifted automatically; manual lifting "gave you more control and felt better." Controls: hold a trigger to lift that foot, use the stick to place it, release to plant it — [Game Rant: How Baby Steps bets everything on a single mechanic](https://gamerant.com/baby-steps-interview-gamble-walking-gameplay-only/); [Game Rant: development interview](https://gamerant.com/baby-steps-development-onterview/)
- Baby Steps' first prototype (2019) was "a couple of boxes for feet, glowing spheres for a butt, and lines for legs," with no ragdoll yet (Foddy) — [Game Rant: Baby Steps prototype](https://gamerant.com/baby-steps-prototype-original-version-game-history-funny/); [Kill Screen interview](https://www.killscreen.com/bennett-foddy-baby-steps-interview/)

**Toribash (Hampus "Hampa" Söderström, Nabi; 2006)**
- Söderström is a Swedish software engineer and judo practitioner. He developed Toribash full-time over about eight months in Singapore, and it was NABI's first title (company formed 2006). It was an IGF 2007 Design Innovation finalist — [Game Developer: Road to the IGF, Toribash](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-i-toribash-i-s-hampus-soderstrom); [Giant Bomb: Nabi Studios](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Companies/Nabi_Studios)
- Control model: 18 joints, each with a single degree of freedom and four states. Earlier schemes used ball joints at the hips, shoulders and torso and were replaced. Co-developer Kristian Saether credits the original idea and most of the code to Söderström — [GameDev.net interview](https://gamedev.net/reference/articles/article2341.asp) (via search summary; page could not be opened)
- Turn-based: players set joint states, then the simulation runs for a short time. The original engine was custom-built, and its "legacy foundation" became an obstacle; Toribash Next is built in Unity — [Epic Games Store: Toribash Next](https://store.epicgames.com/news/how-toribash-next-plans-to-reinvent-the-classic-physics-battler)

**Rain World (Joar Jakobsson and James Therrien, Videocult; 2017)**
- "I never really set out to do procedural animation. I was posed with the problem of making the slugcat's limbs move, and since code is how you make things move in a computer game, that's what I did" — [Unity blog: Exploring procedural design in Rain World: The Watcher (2025)](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)
- Technique: the opposite of Disney-style frame drawing. "I have a bunch of points in space" linked at set distances; the linked points are soft and bendable and adapt to nearly any environment — [Game Developer: Crafting the complex, chaotic ecosystem of Rain World](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-)

- Jakobsson and Therrien gave a GDC 2016 Animation Bootcamp talk on the animation system, later posted free on YouTube — [Game Developer: Video: Animating Rain World](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/video-animating-i-rain-world-i-and-its-many-squishy-stretchy-creatures)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Common recipe in the indie cases: (1) a ragdoll or point-mass body; (2) simple forces such as "keep the head or torso up," "keep the feet under the centre of mass," or "pull the hand toward the aim point"; (3) the player drives a few of those forces directly (arms in HFF, feet in Baby Steps, thighs and calves in QWOP, joint states in Toribash, the hammer in Getting Over It); (4) play-testing decides what stays. Rain World is the 2D, point-and-distance-constraint version of the same idea (Verlet-style chains), which is cheap and robust.
- (Inference) Cutting degrees of freedom was a key breakthrough in several cases: Toribash replaced ball joints with 1-DOF joints, QWOP has four keys, and HFF gives one trigger per arm. Fewer, clearer control channels made a chaotic simulation playable.
- (Inference) "No animator available" was an enabling constraint for Gang Beasts, HFF (solo developer), Sumotori Dreams (96 KB size limit) and Rain World (two-person team). Physics-first movement replaced animation production rather than adding to it.

## Key Question 3: What do developers say about making physics characters controllable, readable and fun rather than chaotic? How did they handle balance, getting up, and blending animation with physics?

### Takeaway
Developers agree that the player must be able to trust the input (a jump is always a jump) and understand why a fall happened. The chaos is welcome only around that reliable core. Too much control kills the comedy (HFF2 "too stiff, too polished"); too little makes the game feel broken (Exanima's patches toward more precise muscles). Balance is usually a cheat-like corrective force (feet pushed under the centre of mass, torso held upright) rather than true biomechanics. AAA games blend: animation drives powered ragdolls through motors with a strength or blend weight, physics takes over during impacts, and control blends back to animation afterwards.

### Cited Findings
- **Trust and readability:** "When you press jump, the character needs to jump. And if they fall over, you should kind of understand why it happened" (Joe Walsh, Fall Guys) — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/fall-guys-interview)
- **Keep the clumsiness:** No Brakes cancelled Human Fall Flat 2 because it was "too stiff. Too polished," on the "wrong branch of physics" — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/-human-fall-flat-2-is-cancelled-we-are-making-human-fall-flat-3-no-brakes-games-founder-looks-back-on-a-defining-decade)
- **Responsive but silly:** Landfall says each project needs "a new physics-based control scheme that feels responsive and fun" while leaving room for emergent humour — [Game Developer: Landfall](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-landfall-games-finds-the-fun-in-physics-engines)
- **Balance as a corrective step:** Landfall's balance script pushes the feet back under the body when both are ahead of the centre of mass — [Game Developer: Landfall](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-landfall-games-finds-the-fun-in-physics-engines)
- **Stable controls are the hardest part:** Baby Steps took "many years of iteration" to get controls that were stable and tight while the physics stayed stable and the motion semi-natural — [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/baby-steps-interview-gamble-walking-gameplay-only/). A critic notes the outcomes are "deliberately imprecise" — [Reverse Shot](https://reverseshot.org/features/3411/baby_steps)
- **Tuning muscle strength and control:** Exanima's developers spent years improving how characters control their "virtual muscles," for smoother, faster and more precise movement, and retuned arm tension during attacks after players found attacks weak and hard to control — [Exanima Steam news](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?feed=steam_community_announcements&appids=362490&enddate=1558211311)
- **Responsiveness first, animation on top:** David Rosen (Wolfire, Overgrowth) builds responsiveness into the physics and procedural layer first, then adds very few keyframes, reportedly about 13 in total, using spring-based interpolation — [GDC Vault: Animation Bootcamp: An Indie Approach to Procedural Animation (2014)](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020049/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach); [Unity Discussions thread](https://discussions.unity.com/t/an-indie-approach-to-procedural-animation-gdc-video-talk/538228); [Polycount discussion](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2093086) (secondary summaries)
- **Partial ragdoll and blend back (Euphoria in GTA IV):** physics takes control for a few seconds, then blends back to motion capture; locomotion stays animation-driven — [AiGameDev](http://aigamedev.com/open/editorial/naturalmotion-euphoria/)
- **Engine-level blending (Unreal):** the Physical Animation Component has a strength multiplier (0 to 1) that scales the motors driving bodies toward the animation pose; the skeletal mesh Physics Blend Weight (1 = fully simulated below a chosen bone, 0 = keyframed) should be set every tick to blend smoothly — [Epic API: UPhysicalAnimationComponent](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/UPhysicalAnimationComponent); [Epic: Physics-driven animation in Unreal Engine](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/physics-driven-animation-in-unreal-engine). Practitioners report keeping blend weight at 1 and controlling influence through strength and profiles to avoid a twitch when entering ragdoll — [Unreal forums](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-to-smoothly-transition-physical-animation-into-ragdoll-v5-2/1237711). Another reports that strength 0 did not fully release the character — [Unreal AnswerHub](https://answers.unrealengine.com/questions/603599/view.html)
- **AAA driven ragdolls:** EA/Frostbite's "driven ragdolls that are able to follow animation and react believably" let teams get emergent physicality "without needing animators to expand animation coverage." The talk covers better animation following, avoiding bad output poses, feeding physics back into the animation system, networking, and generating procedural animation from ragdolls — [GDC Vault: Physics Driven Ragdolls and Animation at EA: From Sports to Star Wars](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025210/Physics-Driven-Ragdolls-and-Animation). Madden NFL 25's BOOM Tech tackles combine "Driven Ragdoll" with other physics and animation systems — [EA: BOOM Tech in Madden NFL 25](https://ea.com/en-us/news/boom-tech-ea-sports-madden-nfl-25)
- **Definitions:** an active ragdoll constrains the animation's output to what a physical system allows, while a simple blended ragdoll binds to the last animation frame and goes limp — [Wikipedia: Ragdoll physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragdoll_physics)

### Inferences
- (Inference) The successful designs have a bimodal control budget: the input channels the player relies on (jump, grab, foot placement, hammer target) are made strong and reliable, while everything else (limb flop, falls, collisions) is left loose for comedy and emergence. HFF2 failed by tightening the loose part; Exanima's patches loosened nothing but made the reliable channels (attacks, muscle control) more dependable.
- (Inference) Getting up is under-documented in public sources. The common practical pattern (Euphoria in GTA, Unreal's blend weight, EA's feedback into the animation system) is to detect a settled or recoverable state, then ramp physics influence down and animation (or motor strength) back up, rather than to simulate a true get-up controller.

- No developer-level description of a get-up controller was found for any indie game listed (Gang Beasts, HFF, TABS, Party Animals, Fall Guys).
- Fall Guys' fall and recovery blending method is not public in anything I found.
- Exanima's balance method is not described; the "low muscle strength on purpose" claim is only from a fan wiki.

## Key Question 4: Which developer talks and articles explain active ragdoll and physical animation techniques step by step?

### Takeaway
The best public step-by-step material is a set of GDC talks: Rosen 2014 (procedural, indie), Rain World 2016 (2D procedural), Naughty Dog 2017 (Uncharted 4 physics animation), EA/Frostbite (driven ragdolls), Respawn 2020 (Jedi: Fallen Order: motors vs velocities vs constraints), Ubisoft (Ragdoll Motion Matching / DReCon, reinforcement learning). ### Inferences
- (Inference) A step-by-step learning path from these sources: (1) build a stable ragdoll with correct joint types and limits (Catto); (2) drive it toward an animated pose with per-joint motors or PD controllers (Unreal component, Unity tutorials, Respawn's motor option); (3) add a balance layer, such as centre-of-mass-over-support stepping (Landfall) or root or torso stabilising forces; (4) blend physics weight per bone and per situation (Unreal blend weight, Euphoria-style takeover then hand-back); (5) optionally replace hand-tuned control with learned policies (DReCon, Ragdoll Motion Matching).
- (Inference) The "god forces" vs "own motors" distinction (Ubisoft) is the key design fork. Most indie games (TABS-style balance forces, upright torques) use external stabilising forces because they are simple and robust. Physically honest systems (DReCon, Euphoria's stated aim, Exanima's "virtual muscles") use only internal joint torques, which look more grounded but are much harder to keep upright.

## Key Question 5 (objective): what is the common pattern for inventing a physics-driven character from nothing?

### Takeaway
Across cases the path is consistent: start from a constraint or a curiosity (no animator, a size limit, a biology question, a new input device); build the crudest physical body that can move; add one or two simple controllers (a pose-tracking motor, an upright force, a foot-placement rule); give the player direct control of a few of those channels; play with it until something is funny or satisfying; then design the rest of the game, and even the character's look, around what the physics does well. Iterate the controller for years, keeping input reliable and leaving everything else loose.

### Cited Findings
- **Constraint or curiosity as the seed:** no animator (Gang Beasts) — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/funny-how-changing-face-comedy-modern-video-games); a hand-tracking controller experiment (HFF) — [PC Games Insider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/success-story/66739/human-fall-flat-shows-that-releasing-a-game-is-just-a-lottery-anyone-can-play-but-you-cant-plan-on-winning/); a biology question (Euphoria) — [Reil & Husbands](https://users.sussex.ac.uk/~philh/pubs/reilhusbandsIEEE.pdf); a 96 KB competition (Sumotori Dreams) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumotori_Dreams); "how do I make the limbs move" (Rain World) — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world); a martial artist unhappy with game combat (Toribash judo, Half Sword HEMA) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-i-toribash-i-s-hampus-soderstrom); [Epic Games Store](https://store.epicgames.com/news/how-half-sword-makes-bloody-brutal-medieval-combat-feel-real)

- **Ideas pass from studio to studio:** Fall Guys drew on Gang Beasts and HFF — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/king-of-the-castle-the-story-behind-the-server-smashing-success-of-fall-guys); Party Animals drew on HFF, Gang Beasts and Stick Fight — [Goomba Stomp](https://goombastomp.com/party-animals-interview/); indie developers combine Rosen's Overgrowth talk with PID research — [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/11835); Half Sword looked to Exanima for armour collisions — [itch.io](https://knives-frank.itch.io/halfsword/comments?after=0)

### Inferences
- (Inference) A reusable pattern for a new project, synthesised from these cases:
  1. **Body:** a few rigid bodies (or point masses in 2D) with limited joints. Fewer degrees of freedom at first.
  2. **Low-level actuator:** per-joint motors or PD/PID controllers that pull toward a target pose (an animation frame, a procedural pose or a player-set state), with a strength limit (the "muscle").
  3. **Balance layer:** cheap corrective rules such as keeping the torso upright, keeping the feet under the centre of mass, and stepping when the centre of mass leaves the support area. Accept "god forces" early and remove them later only if the look demands it.
  4. **Player mapping:** the player directly owns two to four channels (arms, feet, jump, aim). Make those strong and reliable.
  5. **Play and harvest:** test with real players, keep what is funny or satisfying, and design levels and visuals around it.
  6. **Blend and recover:** per-bone physics weight, impact takeover, then ramp back to animation or a target pose for recovery.
  7. **Tune for years:** treat the controller as a living system; protect the "loose" parts that make it charming.
- (Inference, relevant to the owner's avatar work) For a responsive, athletic avatar (as opposed to a comedic flop), the AAA pattern fits better than the indie one: a kinematic or animation controller decides intent and physics follows through motors, with physics weight raised only for impacts and contacts. That is the DReCon, EA driven-ragdoll and Uncharted approach. The indie pattern (physics-first with balance forces) gives charm and emergence but costs precision.
