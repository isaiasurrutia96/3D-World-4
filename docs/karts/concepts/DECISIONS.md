# Kart design decisions and ideas

Recorded from the owner’s November 2026 design sessions for
[the master plan](../../MASTER-PLAN.md). [Current sheets](README.md)

## A. Owner decisions and preferences

1. **Stock direction.** S uses the red wraparound bumper, GT uses the
   selected GT3-inspired direction, and F1 uses the classic formula
   direction. Stock geometry is recorded in the
   [design brief](../../KART-DESIGN.md). Keep the open single center seat
   and the chassis character of each class.
2. **Four tiers for every class.** Standard, Premium, Exotic and Special
   Edition (working name). Classics and rarities can live in Exotic or
   Special Edition. Each themed visual batch uses four base bodies and
   four matching versions showing parts-kit upgrades.
3. **Readable construction.** Each part should look attached to the
   correct spot and be clearly identifiable for Claude. Use a regular
   landscape 2×2 grid for batches. Match the color and view in each
   base and upgraded pair.
4. **GT appearance.** The owner prefers the low, modular, kart-like
   proportions of the yellow-and-black S/GT design studies used for GT
   review. GT race cues can come from the overall form. The original
   compressed street-car shape on GT 27/31 was rejected; the
   exposed-frame revision is the current one.
5. **Racing palettes.** Keep recognizable racing colors and coordinated
   patterns. The owner singled out the burgundy body with pink glow and
   the bronze/copper with light purple pairing on S as clashes. Updated
   sheets use cool white on burgundy, orange with graphite, and other
   coordinated trim and neon combinations.
6. **Show the kit.** The owner asked to show the parts recorded in the
   repo on the upgraded S karts, including clearly visible neon.
   Distribute the parts across builds so the mounting and shape are
   legible. Use the [approved parts kit](../PARTS-KIT.md) for IDs and
   compatibility.

## B. Ideas and experiments for review

1. **GT road-car studies.** The owner explored GT3 cues, Japanese tuners,
   American performance cars and the red 2014 Civic Si. The archive
   keeps both roofed and kart-style studies. Future game models favor
   original names, badges and bodywork derived from broad racing cues.
2. **Manufacturer collaboration.** The owner raised an optional licensed
   kart sold in-game with a sales royalty. It remains a proposal.
   The names, approved art, customization, sale terms and any
   co-marketing would be agreed if the idea is pursued.
3. **Open-world use cases.** Gravel, night driving, street tuning and
   hillclimb appeared as GT themes. These are visual concepts the owner
   can use when reviewing which activities belong in the open world.
