# 15 — Level and World Design

## A) Nintendo's four-step structure (kishōtenketsu)

Koichi Hayashida (director, Super Mario 3D World) designs each level around one
idea in four beats, borrowed from Japanese story structure:

1. **Introduce (ki):** show the mechanic in a safe place with no penalty.
2. **Develop (shō):** use it in harder or combined situations.
3. **Twist (ten):** change the context so it's used in a surprising way.
4. **Conclude (ketsu):** a final test or celebration, then move on to a new
   idea. Mechanics are rarely reused heavily, so every level feels fresh.

## B) Teaching without text

1. First encounter in a safe spot, failure costs little.
2. Use the level shape to guide: light, color, and open paths draw the eye;
   coins/collectibles form breadcrumb trails.
3. Show, then let the player do it (an enemy demonstrates a hazard first).

## C) 3D platformer worlds

1. **Super Mario 64:** each course is a sandbox with seven star missions whose
   names hint at the goal; players revisit the same space with new purposes.
   Vertical space matters as much as horizontal.
2. **Super Mario Odyssey:** small but **dense** kingdoms packed with secrets
   (900+ moons); collectibles are along the road rather than the destination;
   the capture mechanic encourages poking at everything. Critics note some
   moons feel padded — density needs quality.
3. **Landmarks:** a tall, visible object in every area helps orientation.
4. **Readable surfaces:** walkable vs. deadly vs. climbable should look
   different.

## D) Multiplayer and arena maps

1. Three-lane layouts for shooters (see `08-shooters.md`).
2. Symmetry for fairness, or balanced asymmetry.
3. Sightlines: mix long and short; break long ones with cover.
4. Loops so players never hit dead ends.

## E) Sports venues

1. Golf holes: tee, fairway, hazards (bunkers, water), green; each hole poses a
   risk/reward choice (cut the corner over water or play safe).
2. Mini golf: one gimmick per hole (ramp, loop, moving obstacle), escalating.
3. Race tracks: readable turns, shortcuts that reward skill, landmarks per
   sector, recovery after crashes.

## F) Open worlds

1. Density over size (see `09-open-world.md`).
2. Points of interest spaced so something new is visible every ~30–60 seconds
   of travel.
3. Systems that combine (Breath of the Wild's physics + chemistry) create
   emergent play without authoring every moment.
