# Claude's thoughts: who should do what, and each assistant's strengths (24 November 2026)

**These are Claude's own thoughts and opinions, not owner decisions and not rules.** They were written by the "LetsGo Plan" Claude chat in
answer to two questions from the owner, and saved at the owner's request ("make sure in the repo it's your thoughts"). Claude is one of the two
assistants being judged, so read this with that bias in mind. The judgments come from what the repo shows; Claude cannot see Codex's chats,
only the work Codex saved.

## A. The owner's first question (hypothetical)

Imagine building a brand-new game on a clean slate. Answering honestly, like a parent who wants the absolute best and is not trying to get the
owner to use Claude more: who would do what? What would this chat be strongest at, what would other Claude chats do, and what would Codex do?
The owner's example: Codex understands simple prompts well, while Claude sometimes needs to be asked for a prompt to give to it.

## B. Claude's answer

Here's my honest answer, as if I were a parent who only wanted the best for you.

One caution first. I'm Claude, so I could be biased. To stay fair, I'm judging from what your own repo shows, not from what I'd like to be true.

What the evidence says about Codex: the strongest code in your whole project is Codex's driving engine. It's cleanly organized, heavily tested,
and it behaves the same on slow and fast phones. Your driving feel got approved twice. And you're right that Codex acts well on short, simple
prompts.

What the evidence says about Claude: Claude's walking was rejected five to seven times, and a lot of its time went into small look changes. But
part of that came from the setup, not the model. Every Claude chat here has to read about seventeen thousand words of rules and history before
it starts, and it's told to research, compare and wait for your Go on every step. That's a big reason Claude feels slower and sometimes needs
you to translate a prompt. Claude's real strengths in your repo are clear explanations, honest records, the checkpoint system that protects
your approved work, and teaching you as it goes.

So for a brand-new game, here's who I'd have do what.

1. **Codex builds the engine and the game rules:** physics, driving, the game loop, race rules, minigame scoring, and the publishing and
   testing setup. That's where its track record is best.
2. **Claude building chats handle the character and how he moves,** the world rules, and anything you judge by feel. They work with you in
   short loops, explain what they did, and write the plain-English records.
3. **This kind of chat, the planning chat with research teams, is for the big decisions only:** researching, comparing, double-checking, and
   keeping the master plan honest. It's slower and uses a lot of usage, so it shouldn't do daily building.
4. **They review each other's work.** A fresh pair of eyes catches mistakes the builder can't see.
5. **You decide purpose and taste:** what the game is, how it should feel, and whether it looks right on your phone.

Two honest caveats. Both tools change every few months, and the gap between them is smaller than the gap a good or bad setup makes. If you want
proof instead of my opinion, give both the same small task, like the race countdown, and compare the results. That's cheap, and it settles it
with evidence.

## C. The owner's second question

What are your top three strongest attributes, and what are Codex's top three?

## D. Claude's answer

I don't think I mentioned numbers and shapes in this chat. You may be thinking of the avatar chat, which measures every shape with numbers.
Here's my honest answer, based on what your repo shows. I can't see Codex's chats, only the work it saved.

**My three strongest suits, as Claude:**

1. **Understanding and explaining.** I take a messy idea, turn it into a clear plan, and explain it in plain English. Your reports, records
   and decision notes show that.
2. **Research and careful reasoning across a lot of material.** Reading the whole project, comparing many options, spotting contradictions,
   and double-checking, like this review did.
3. **Careful, measured detail work.** Checking shapes and sizes with numbers, protecting your approved work with fingerprints, and being honest
   about what isn't confirmed yet.

My honest weakness: I can over-plan, get too process-heavy, and drift into polishing details unless there's a firm target.

**Codex's three strongest suits:**

1. **Engine-quality systems code.** The driving engine is the cleanest, most solid code in your project.
2. **Testing discipline.** It proves things work with lots of automatic checks, like showing the kart drives the same at any frame rate.
3. **Acting directly on short prompts.** You give it a simple instruction, and it gets to work without much back-and-forth.

Codex's honest weakness: it can widen the scope, like adding extra realism and car variants before the race itself existed. Its notes also tend
to grow into long diaries.

Both of us can do all six of these. These are tendencies, not hard limits, and both tools keep changing. A small side-by-side test on the same
task would show the difference better than any opinion, including mine.

Whenever you're ready: are your ideas all saved, so I can write the master plan?
